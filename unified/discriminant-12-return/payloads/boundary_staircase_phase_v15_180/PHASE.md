# Could a graviton be a gravitational phase relation?

Sandbox addendum, 2026-10-29. Jeremy's question after v15.140.
Producer `phase_relation_test.py` (finite quarter ring, clean run).

## The formalisable content [D, finite model]
On the quarter ring (N = 24, U = quarter-step, P = even-sector
projector), two families of states are exactly complementary:
- **Sector states** — site states and H_EM eigenstates inside a sector:
  even weight 1, and ⟨U⟩ = 0 exactly. Everything EM dynamics can prepare
  carries *no* inter-sector phase.
- **Phase-relation states** — eigenstates of U itself (Bloch waves on
  the quarter grid): even weight exactly 1/2, odd weight 1/2, and ⟨U⟩ a
  pure phase (|⟨U⟩| = 1 exactly). Their entire content is the relative
  phase between rungs and across the sector boundary.

So "a gravitational phase relation" is a well-defined excitation type in
the ladder's own language: a quantum of the relative phase between the
grid's two sectors — invisible to H_EM (which preserves sectors), and
exactly what the gravity operator U measures. It is not a multiplet
occupant: it lives across both sectors at once, which is the only place
left after v15.140's boundary result (the odd sector hosts no unitary
particle multiplets; the even sector's multiplets have ⟨U⟩ = 0).

## Rhymes already on the record [I]
- The Misner string is a patch phase relation; its single-valuedness
  quantisation is the ħ/4 of v14.731.
- The monopole is the offset: a relative phase (twist s in Π_φ = p_φ +
  s cosθ) between the two parabolic channels.
- The graviton's observable content in v15.062 is the helicity pair's
  relative phase: Q + iU = 2√(uv) e^{iΦ} on the Stokes/Poincaré sphere.
- Gravitational waves are in practice detected only as phase relations
  (interferometer arm-phase differences), and single-graviton detection
  is the famously impossible one (Rothman–Boughn).

## Scope
[I], a reinterpretation, not a displacement of the QFT graviton: the
standard graviton is an even-sector Fock state (j = 2, v15.140 §3) and
nothing here touches its consistency. The claim is only that on the
cone's grid, gravity's *characteristic* excitation — the one with no
EM-sector analogue — is a phase relation between sectors rather than a
state in a sector. Consistency with detection practice is noted as
consistency, not evidence.
