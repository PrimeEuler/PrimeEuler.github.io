# Position on the hyperbola = the opposite-sign parabola crossing

Producer: `state_crossing.py` / `state_crossing_output.txt`.

A mesh crossing (n, k), read as E = AM = (n+k)/2, p = offset =
(n−k)/2, m = GM = √(nk), satisfies E² − p² = nk = m² identically
(the v14.550 diameter–secant identity): **every crossing lies on
the boost hyperbola labelled by its own GM, and its position
along that hyperbola is the pair's ratio**, ψ = ½ ln(n/k) from
the seat (n = k). The two opposite-sign parabolas meeting at the
crossing (v15.235) carry the light-cone components u = E + pc,
v = E − pc as labels, with product uv = (mc²)² fixed; a boost is
the product-preserving split (u, v) → (λu, v/λ).

Electron (v15.272's hyperbola, continuous labels): at rest
u = v = 0.51100 MeV — the diagonal crossing, the opposed pair in
exact balance, ψ = 0. At E = 1 MeV the pair is (1.85958, 0.14042),
ψ = 1.2917 = arccosh(E/m); at 5 MeV, (9.97382, 0.02618),
ψ = 2.9714. Rest is balance; motion is imbalance at fixed product.

Seams: the electron's labels are continuous — the parabola
families foliate continuously, the integer mesh samples them; and
on the integer mesh the seat crossing exists only for square
labels (N = 36 has its rest point; N = 12 and N = 60 are sampled
at every factor pair except exactly at rest). The continuum pair
always has its seat.

A state's full address in this structure: one hyperbola (mass =
the product) and on it one opposite-sign crossing (position =
the ratio). Nothing else is needed, and nothing else is there.
