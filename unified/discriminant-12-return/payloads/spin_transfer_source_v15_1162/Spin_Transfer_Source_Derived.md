## The source and the derived gradient

Two infinite straight wires parallel to ŷ at
(x, z) = (±a, 0), carrying equal currents +I, plus a
Helmholtz pair supplying B0. Biot–Savart gives the
wire-pair field in closed form; it is exactly
divergence- and curl-free in the measurement region
(verified symbolically), and its Taylor expansion at
the center is precisely v15.1131's field,

    B = (Gz, 0, Gx) + O(3),   with   G = μ₀I/(πa²)

— the imported coefficient is the leading term of an
explicit source, with μ₀, the current I and the wire
separation a as its inputs. The Helmholtz pair's
on-axis field is even about the center, so B0 arrives
gradient-free, as the chain assumes.

## The exact transfer

Because the source field is known exactly, the sensor
splitting no longer needs the linearized gradient. At
the filed sensor positions r_A = −r_B
= (d/2)(cosθ, sinθ, 0):

    ΔΩ_exact(θ) = γGd·cosθ / (1 − (d·cosθ/(2a))²).

v15.1131's γGd·cosθ is the leading term in d/a
(series-verified); the first correction is the exact
factor's (d·cosθ/(2a))² — at d/(2a) = 0.1, a ≤1%
correction, now computable rather than neglected.
Assembling the full transfer (tidal torque →
susceptibility → ∂θΔΩ_exact → Ramsey window, per
Gates 1031/1035) with G = μ₀I/(πa²): the transfer
vanishes identically at I = 0. The operator's
off-switch sits exactly at the laboratory input — the
coil current — which is where an import of this kind
physically belongs: the experimenter supplies a
current, not a field coefficient.
