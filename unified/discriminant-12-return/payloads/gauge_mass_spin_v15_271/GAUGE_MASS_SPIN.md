# Mass as orbit label, spin transport as the gauge; J in atomic EM structure

Producer: `gauge_mass_spin.py` / `gauge_mass_spin_output.txt`.

**Labels.** (m, J) are the two Poincaré Casimir labels (Wigner):
gauge-invariant data, not gauge choices. The mass shell
E² − p²c² = (mc²)² is a Lorentz orbit; mass selects the orbit,
J labels the little-group representation on it. Same grammar as
the record's trajectory cones: the pair charge s selects the cone
at fixed |J| = j + ½ (v15.229, v15.230) — a scalar label picks the
surface, spin lives on it.

**The gauge content.** (i) The rest point on the orbit is a frame
choice. (ii) Spin transport along the orbit: boosts do not
commute, so a carried spin acquires a Wigner rotation; the
continuous form is Thomas precession — a connection on the mass
shell with measurable holonomy. (iii) In the charge sectors the
Dirac (EM) / Misner (TN) string placement is the gauge freedom,
while the product eg = s is the invariant label.

**Atomic EM structure measures both.** Dirac hydrogen levels
depend on j through |κ| = j + ½: the 2P3/2 − 2P1/2 splitting is
4.528e-5 eV = 10.95 GHz (measured 10.97 GHz). Inside that number
sits the Thomas half: naive rest-frame spin-orbit coefficient
g/2 = 1.00116; Thomas precession subtracts 1/2; observed
(g−1)/2 = 0.50116 (without the transport correction the splitting
is twice too large). And hydrogen's parabolic states |n₁n₂m⟩ are
the mesh crossings (Paper C): the mesh offset k = n₁ − n₂ = 2X is
the Stark quantum number, with linear Stark shift
ΔE = (3/2)nkF — n = 2: ±3F, 0; n = 3: ±9F, ±4.5F, 0 — so the grid
offset is an atomic EM observable.

**Seam.** Labels plus transport constrain how J states ride a
chosen hyperbola; they do not select which (m, J) occur.
