# K4 AM-GM geometry: exact radial flux and the missing charge

Date 2026-10-26. Parents v14.818, v14.820–821, v14.919–921, v14.941 and latest Lane A v14.942–943 (read-only). Scope: mathematical metric with conditional conserved current; Suzuki–1/4 integration parked.

Metric ds²=dτ²−a(τ)²[dχ²+χ²dΩ²], a=τ/√3; √−g=a³χ²sinθ, R=aχ, A=4πR².

For arbitrary spherically symmetric Jμ, ∇μJμ=0 iff ∂τ(a³χ²Jτ)+∂χ(a³χ²Jχ)=0. For a purely radial conserved spatial current with Jτ=0 (kinematic conserved flux, not a physical timelike number four-current), radial integration shows a³χ²Jχ=C(τ). Physical orthonormal radial component Jhatχ=aJχ=C/(a²χ²)=4πC/A. Define Φ(τ)=4π C(τ), obtain Jhatχ=Φ(τ)/(4πR²). Conservation alone does not demand Φ independent of τ; no global stationarity exists in the expanding metric.

For an actually causal outgoing null number current one may take retarded conformal coordinate u=η−χ, dη=dτ/a, and Jτ=N f(u)/(4π a³χ²), Jχ=Jτ/a (for any smooth nonnegative f). Then ∇J=0 exactly off source, JμJμ=0, and each outward shell has an inverse-area dilution factor with additional a/time factors. Its arbitrary normalization N and profile f are input, not fixed by Hessian AM-GM.

A physically familiar stronger instance requires additional electromagnetic gauge dynamics (not from AM-GM alone). For Maxwell ∇μF^{μν}=J^ν/ε0 and an isolated conserved central electric charge Q, Gauss law on a fixed τ slice gives physical radial electric field Ehatχ=Q/[4πε0(aχ)²]=Q/(4πε0R²), provided appropriate normalization and no other charges. The factor Q is externally supplied by matter/gauge charge, not determined by the ball/cube ratio π/6, exponent 1/3, or the product Hessian. Gauss law and area yield the inverse-square dependence in ordinary spatially flat FLRW too; no unique gravitational discovery or Newton's G established.

The point-source lightfront has R_light=τ ln(τ/τe) from v14.919. Conditional flux density on that sphere is Φ(τ)/(4πτ²ln²(τ/τe)). If the outgoing current is a null pulse rather than a steady spatial flux, emission/observation rates must be treated with retarded time and cosmological time dilation; do not conflate the two.
