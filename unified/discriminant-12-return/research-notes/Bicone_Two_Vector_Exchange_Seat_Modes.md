# Bicone two-vector exchange, magnetic modes, and central-seat coupling

Date 2026-10-25. Parents v13.595, v14.097–099, v14.856–861. Gravity/bicone research note. Additive only; protected Suzuki and far-tail untouched.

## Exact SU(2) exchange spectrum [D, conditional Hamiltonian]
For two independent spin-j sectors, \(J_1²=J_2²=j(j+1)\) and \(L=J_1+J_2\), \(A=J_1-J_2\), define the POSTULATED rotationally invariant exchange interaction \(H_{\rm ex}=-\lambda J_1\cdot J_2\). This exchange coupling is not derived from the earlier bicone SO(4,2) dipole construction, and λ has energy units (operators dimensionless). Since \(J_1\cdot J_2=[L²-2j(j+1)]/2\),
\[
E_L=-\frac{\lambda}{2}[L(L+1)-2j(j+1)],\quad L=0,\ldots,2j.
\]
For λ>0 ferromagnetic ground multiplet L=2j, the nearest lower multiplet L=2j-1 has gap \(2\lambda j\) (j>0). For λ<0 the ground L=0, first excitation L=1 has gap |λ|. Uniform driver \(H_B=-\Omega L_z\) commutes with \(L²\), so it does not excite the relative-L gap. A staggered/differential field \(H_{\rm rel}=-g\delta A_x\) can couple total-L sectors; under exchange of the two spins, A is odd and L is even. Selection: A is a rank-one vector and changes L by ±1 for equal-spin exchange parity, not arbitrary ΔL.

Classical aligned rigid-length vectors |J1|=|J2|=j separated by small angle θ have \(-\lambda J_1\cdot J_2=-\lambda j²\cos θ\), so the *relative angular* energy excess is \(+\lambda j² θ²/2+O(θ⁴)\) for λ>0. This is an internal restoring TORQUE for relative orientation. A uniform rigid rotation of both vectors costs zero exchange energy, so it passes the earlier objectivity test. The exchange-curvature result is not automatically a seat-displacement stiffness.

## Dynamical mode contrast [D]
Under a uniform field -ΩL_z, the linearized classical two-spin precession equations around J1=J2=j zhat have a common circular-polarization mode with frequency Ω, and a relative circular-polarization mode with frequency Ω+2λj (signs depend on gyromagnetic precession conventions; magnitudes are quoted for Ω,λ>0). These are conservative oscillatory/precessional modes, not damping or relaxation. A rotating transverse magnetic drive coupling to Lx,Ly acts in the common sector at linear order, whereas a differential perturbation coupling to Ax,Ay excites the relative sector. Thus the magnetic-drive implementation must explain how the parabolic-seat displacement generates a differential rather than purely uniform field.

## j=1/2 exact diagnostic [D]
Triplet L=1 has exchange energy -λ/4; singlet L=0 has +3λ/4, hence gap λ. Add uniform bias \(-\Omega L_z\) (Ω>0) and choose |triplet,m=+1> as the nondegenerate ground if λ>0; its energy E_t=-λ/4-Ω. A transverse differential seat perturbation \(V=-g\delta A_x\) connects |t,+1> to the singlet with matrix element of magnitude 1/sqrt2, while the singlet is at E_s=3λ/4. In the {|t,+1>,|s>} channel the second-order ground energy shift is
\[
\Delta E_g=-\frac{g²\delta²}{2(\lambda+\Omega)}+O(\delta^4)
\]
if other matrix elements are absent from that state, as they are for Ax acting on |t,+1>. Hence seat curvature contribution \(E_g''(0)=-g²/(\lambda+\Omega)\), NEGATIVE. Exchange supplies a positive restoring energy against a prescribed relative ANGLE but the ground-state differential-field susceptibility SOFTENS a coordinate which linearly drives the relative operator. They cannot be conflated.

## Open test [O]
To convert relative angle θ into AM–GM seat displacement δ, derive an explicit and symmetry-consistent map θ(δ) from bicone geometry and magnetic driving, then evaluate total potential curvature including geometric/bond terms. Factor grid u=1/2 vs 1 scales the mapping but does not select λ, Ω, or g. Need independent action/state preparation, physical rotational coupling, and full driver dynamics. No G/α inference.
