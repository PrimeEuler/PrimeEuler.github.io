# Covariant elastic solid candidate for Cone w=-1/3 background

2026-10-24. Gravity subgate, parents v14.822, v14.826–829. Signature +---. No edits to protected Suzuki/far-tail.

Let φ^I (I=1,2,3) be comoving scalar material labels; define B^{IJ}=-g^{μν}∂μφ^I∂νφ^J, b=(det B)^{1/2}, and q=tr B/[3(det B)^{1/3}]-1. Choose a fixed internal Euclidean δ_IJ for the trace. On the positive-definite B sector, arithmetic-geometric mean implies q≥0, with equality at B∝I. Let
\[
S=\int\sqrt{-g}[R/(2κ)-F(b,q)]d⁴x,\qquad F=b^{2/3}(C+Dq),\ C>0,\ D\ge0.
\]
This is generally covariant with an internal rotation symmetry of the three labels. It is an isotropic solid rather than a volume-preserving-relabeling-symmetric perfect fluid.

On flat FLRW with φ^I=z^I, B=a^-2 I, b=a^-3, q=0. Because q has vanishing first variation at isotropy, the shear term does not change background stress. Thus ρ=C a^-2, p=-ρ/3 and Einstein equations give a(τ)=sqrt(κC/3)(τ-τ0) in the expanding branch. This reproduces the background of v14.821 when κC=1.

For perturbative strains in a local comoving inertial frame with B normalized to I, let φ^I=x^I+π^I. To second order the shape invariant is q=(2/3) e_dev:e_dev+O(π³), with e=sym ∇π, after accounting for the isotropic volume deformation. Thus the quadratic shear-energy coefficient is μ=2Db^(2/3)/3=2Dρ/(3C). The inertial density is ρ+p=2ρ/3. Standard relativistic isotropic-solid principal mode speeds are
\[
c_T²=μ/(\rho+p)=D/C,\quad
c_L²=dp/dρ+4μ/[3(\rho+p)]=-1/3+4D/(3C).
\]
Therefore local nonnegative subluminal conditions 0≤c_T²,c_L²≤1 hold for 1/4≤D/C≤1. At D/C=1/4 the longitudinal speed is zero (marginal principal stability); a strict positive longitudinal speed needs D/C>1/4. At D/C=1 the longitudinal speed is luminal (boundary). A convenient interior example D/C=1/2 gives c_T²=1/2, c_L²=1/3. These are leading local material characteristics in the isotropic background, not full cosmic-mode stability or strong-coupling certificates.

This action does not include the microscopic rotor degrees of freedom or derive D from v14.829's J, K, A, gamma. Matching macroscopic shear μ to an effective lattice modulus requires a coarse-graining derivation and units; nothing here predicts D/C or Newton G. Gravity TT action retains the positive Einstein-Hilbert principal kinetic coefficient for κ>0, but solid anisotropic stress can modify subleading tensor evolution; full scalar/vector/tensor constraint and Hamiltonian stability remain open.
