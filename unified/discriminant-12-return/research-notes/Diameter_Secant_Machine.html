① The circle of diameter n+k

n segment k segment GM = √(nk), the half-chord AM = (n+k)/2, the radius offset (n−k)/2.
The angle φ at the center has cos φ = (n−k)/(n+k): it is the argument of zn,k. At fixed k, the chord's endpoint walks the faint parabola — vertex at −k/2, focal length k/2.

② The GM hyperbola u² − v² = nk

The secant's foot, lifted: u = AM horizontal, v = offset vertical. The dashed 45° lines are the light-cone: from the point, u+v = n and u−v = k — the pair is the light-cone coordinates. The ray from the origin is the rapidity ψ = ½ ln(n/k): tanh ψ = (n−k)/(n+k), coth ψ = (n+k)/(n−k).

Live readouts

How to read it. Euclid's altitude theorem: the altitude to the hypotenuse of a right triangle is the geometric mean of the two segments it makes. Thales' semicircle makes the hypotenuse a diameter, so the altitude is the half-chord — the GM, drawn as a length. Ptolemy ran planetary kinematics on half-chords; Galileo's trajectories are the parabolas; the hyperbola is the mass shell, and the rapidities add. The assembly isn't in the literature — the parts are ~2,300 years old.
