For any smooth periodic scalar potential b on flat T³, β^i=∂^i b, Sij=(∂iβj+∂jβi)/2=∂i∂j b, θ=∂iβi=Δb. Fourier: θ_hat(K)=−|K|² bhat(K), Sij_hat(K)=−Ki Kj bhat(K). Therefore Σij |Sij_hat|²=|K|⁴|bhat|²=|θ_hat|², and ⟨θ²−SijSij⟩=0 exactly for real b. Equivalent divergence: (Δb)²−|Hess b|²=∂i[(∂i b)Δb−(∂j b)(∂i∂j b)] (check differentiation and commuting partials). This *does not vanish pointwise* and is not true of arbitrary vector β without longitudinal restriction, though under periodic integration any β gradient-square combination may have other properties.

Hence full averaged H2 of v15.109 simplifies on scalar first-order modes by deleting ⟨θ²−SijSij⟩, without assuming α, ψ, π or dotπ coefficients or full Hamiltonian solution. Other mixed shift terms (−12H⟨ψθ⟩+4⟨dotψθ⟩+8H⟨αθ⟩) and |dotπ−β|² remain. This is an exact algebraic cancellation of one problematic quadratic sector, not zero-mode cancellation of full H2. In R³, same holds under enough decay/boundary flux conditions. On T³ zero shift K=0 mode of b is irrelevant (gradient zero), nonzero constant shift corresponds a harmonic vector not of periodic gradient form; handle separately.
