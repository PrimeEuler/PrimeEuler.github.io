# Scalar focus versus first magnetic ladder transition

Date 2026-10-25. Parents v13.595, v14.620, v14.767, v14.864. Gravity/magnetic-drive side lane. No Suzuki/far-tail edits.

## Exact edge geometry [D]
In spin j, J+|j,m> = sqrt((j-m)(j+m+1))|j,m+1>. For j>=1/2 the first edge m=-j -> -j+1 and last edge m=j-1 -> j both have squared amplitude 2j. Centered coordinate X=m+1/2 gives for first edge X=1/2-j, last X=j-1/2. Hence eliminate j:
first Y²=1-2X, last Y²=1+2X. These share focus (X,Y)=(0,0), with vertices X=+1/2 and X=-1/2, and meet at (0,1), the j=1/2 single edge.

## Spin-zero limit [D]
At j=0 the representation has one state |0,0>, all generators Jx,Jy,Jz,J± act as zero, and no upward/downward edge exists. Algebraically extending the outer-edge formula Y²=2j to j=0 places the first/last extrapolated edge vertices at X=+1/2 and -1/2, not at the common focus. Thus the physical j=0 scalar representation is NOT a point on an existing transition ladder, and it does not coincide with the focus through the edge formulas. Calling the shared focus a "spin-zero seat" is a geometric interpretive identification, not an operator-theoretic equality.

## u scaling [D]
Under common factor-coordinate dilation (X,Y)->u(X,Y), the parabolas become Y²=u²±2uX, with vertices ±u/2 and common focus 0. The first j=1/2 edge is (0,u); u=1/2 produces (0,1/2). Relative transition-amplitude square scales as u² by coordinate convention, whereas the dimensionless SU(2) matrix element remains 1. This change does not independently establish a gravitational operator/coupling or physical spin-quarter ladder.

## Open [O]
A true focus-to-scalar operator must be specified: e.g. representation-changing map from V0 into V1/2, which J± cannot accomplish because they preserve j. In a bicone with two equal j sectors, j=0⊗j=0 vacuum and the total-spin L=0 singlet within j=1/2⊗j=1/2 are different states in different number sectors. A pair-creation/annihilation operator, with appropriate symmetry, could connect such sectors, but requires explicit construction and Hamiltonian.
