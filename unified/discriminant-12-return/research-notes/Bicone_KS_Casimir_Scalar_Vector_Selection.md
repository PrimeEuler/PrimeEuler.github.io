# KS bicone Casimir test of scalar and vector sectors

Date 2026-10-25. Parents v14.680, v14.864–868. Additive bicone/gravity lane only.

Let J1,J2 be mutually commuting su(2) generators, each of fixed magnitude j(j+1) within the KS-glued oscillator sector Na=Nb=2j. Define L=J1+J2 and A=J1-J2. Operator identities:
L²+A²=2(J1²+J2²)=4j(j+1) I;
L·A=J1²-J2²=0 (components commute across factors).
[L_i,L_j]=i ε_ijk L_k, [L_i,A_j]=i ε_ijk A_k, [A_i,A_j]=i ε_ijk L_k. The so(4) algebra distinguishes rotational L and relative Runge–Lenz-like A, but the notation A does not establish physical normalization of the hydrogen Runge–Lenz vector.

For j=1/2, the glued n=2 sector has four states, decomposing into one L=0 singlet and L=1 triplet. Exact values:
L=0: L²=0, A²=3;
L=1: L²=2, A²=1.
The scalar has nonzero A² even though <A>=0 in a rotationally invariant singlet, and relative-vector operator A may connect scalar ↔ vector. Matrix elements, not just squared invariant, decide observable response.

KS Q=Na-Nb=0 holds in both sectors. The free bicone oscillator H0=ℏω(Na+Nb+2)=2nℏω on glued n shell commutes with L² and A² and is exactly degenerate for n=2. The Coulomb mapping from v14.680 gives E_n=-μκ²/(2ℏ²n²) for every state on n shell; hence scalar n=2 s-wave and vector n=2 p-wave share the ideal nonrelativistic Coulomb energy (before fine structure/Lamb shifts). This does not identify the scalar with the Higgs: L=0 is orbital SO(3) rotational angular momentum here, not the intrinsic Lorentz-spin quantum number of a fundamental particle.

S† pair creator maps oscillator vacuum n=1 (hydrogen 1s analogue) into n=2 L=0 state (2s analogue), while symmetric pair creators create n=2 L=1 triplet (2p analogue). Bare electric-dipole vector selection from 1s reaches 2p but not 2s. The pair creator S† is not established as a physical electromagnetic transition operator. The Coulomb spectrum's κ remains external; the gravitational analogue is unproven.

An L² term cL² or exchange -λJ1·J2 would split scalar/vector states but it is an additional dynamical coupling, not part of the KS constraint or ideal Coulomb Hamiltonian. No Higgs mass, common EM/G monopole coupling, or Newton G follows.
