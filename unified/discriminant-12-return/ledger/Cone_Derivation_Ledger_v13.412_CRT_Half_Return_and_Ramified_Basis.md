## 1. Canonical return in the two audited ramified-ideal bases [D]

The principal paper uses the ramified ideal

\[
\mathfrak p_2=(2,\sqrt3-1)
\]

with basis

\[
f_1=2,\qquad f_2=\sqrt3-1,
\]

for which multiplication by \(\lambda=2+\sqrt3\) is

\[
[g_{12}]_f=
\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It also uses

\[
e_1=1+\sqrt3,\qquad e_2=3+\sqrt3,
\]

with basis-change matrix

\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
C^{-1}g_{12}C=
\begin{pmatrix}2&3\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus the familiar Pell matrix and the paper's canonical positive-integral return are the same unit action in two integral bases.

## 2. The scalar half-return is basis-independent [D]

From v13.411 (originally v13.406),

\[
g_{12}^6\equiv7I\pmod{12},
\qquad
g_{12}^{12}\equiv I\pmod{12}.
\]

Because \(C\in GL_2(\mathbf Z)\) has determinant \(-1\), its reduction modulo 12 is invertible. Therefore

\[
C^{-1}(7I)C=7I
\]

and hence the same half-return appears in the \((e_1,e_2)\) Pell basis:

\[
\boxed{
(C^{-1}g_{12}C)^6\equiv7I\pmod{12}.}
\]

So the occurrence of \(7I\) is not an artifact of the chosen ramified-ideal coordinates. More generally, any invertible change of basis fixes a scalar matrix under conjugation.

## 3. Exact CRT decomposition of the 12-step return [D]

Reducing the canonical return separately modulo 3 and modulo 4 gives:

### Modulo 3

\[
\boxed{g_{12}^3\equiv -I\pmod3},
\qquad
\boxed{g_{12}^6\equiv I\pmod3}.
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{ord}_3(g_{12})=6.
\]

### Modulo 4

\[
\boxed{g_{12}^2\equiv -I\pmod4},
\qquad
\boxed{g_{12}^4\equiv I\pmod4}.
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{ord}_4(g_{12})=4.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{12}(g_{12})
=\operatorname{lcm}(6,4)=12.
\]

At the global half-return \(n=6\), the two CRT components are

\[
g_{12}^6\equiv I\pmod3,
\qquad
g_{12}^6\equiv -I\pmod4.
\]

The unique scalar \(r\pmod{12}\) satisfying

\[
r\equiv1\pmod3,
\qquad
r\equiv-1\pmod4
\]

is

\[
\boxed{r=7}. 
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{g_{12}^6\equiv7I\pmod{12}}
\]

has a precise CRT meaning: it is the splice of **identity on the mod-3 component** with **sign reversal on the mod-4 component**.

This is stronger than merely observing the number 7 in a power table.

## 4. Relation to the already-audited mod-2 Frobenius layer [D]

Modulo 2, in the ramified basis,

\[
\bar g_{12}=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\]

which has order 2. After the audited additive identification

\[
f_1\mapsto1,\qquad f_2\mapsto\omega^2
\]

with \(\mathbf F_4\), this transported action is Frobenius.

This finite-field action is a quotient of the mod-2 coordinate reduction and must remain distinct from literal residue multiplication by the Pell unit, which is trivial in the cyclotomic residue field because

\[
\lambda=2+\sqrt3\equiv1.
\]

The new CRT statement does not change that guardrail. It only refines the higher mod-4 information that is invisible after reducing all the way to mod 2.

In particular,

\[
g_{12}^2\equiv -I\pmod4
\]

reduces further to

\[
g_{12}^2\equiv I\pmod2,
\]

exactly as expected from the order-2 Frobenius reduction.

## 5. Interpretation of the half-return [D/E]

The global residue period 12 is built from two central sign structures:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{modulus} & \text{central sign step} & \text{full period}\\
\hline
3 & g^3=-I & 6\\
4 & g^2=-I & 4
\end{array}}
\]

The global step \(n=6\) is simultaneously

- a full return modulo 3;
- an odd number of mod-4 half-turns, hence \(-I\) modulo 4.

CRT therefore yields the central scalar \(7I\) modulo 12.

**[E] Working interpretation:** the distinguished residue \(7\) is not generated by geometric motion among the V4 shells. Rather, it is the canonical CRT sign character of the Pell return at half of its global mod-12 period: \(+1\) on the mod-3 factor and \(-1\) on the mod-4 factor.

This gives a representation-independent arithmetic meaning to the previously observed \(7I\) half-return.

## 6. V4 comparison [D]

Under CRT,

\[
U(12)\cong U(3)\times U(4)\cong C_2\times C_2.
\]

The four units correspond to sign pairs

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
r & r\bmod3 & r\bmod4\\
\hline
1 & +1 & +1\\
5 & -1 & +1\\
7 & +1 & -1\\
11 & -1 & -1
\end{array}
\]

Therefore the Pell half-return selects exactly the V4 element

\[
\boxed{7\leftrightarrow(+1,-1)}.
\]

This is an exact connection between the cyclic Pell residue dynamics and the CRT realization of \(U(12)\cong V_4\), but it remains only an **intersection** of symmetry layers:

- the four V4 labels in the Cone figure are distinct shells;
- the Pell boost preserves each shell;
- the cyclic Pell return does not generate all four scalar V4 elements;
- its scalar intersection with \(U(12)\) remains \(\{1,7\}\).
