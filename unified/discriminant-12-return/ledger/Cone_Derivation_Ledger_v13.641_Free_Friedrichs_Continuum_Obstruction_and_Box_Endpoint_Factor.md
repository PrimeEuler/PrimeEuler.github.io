## 1. Operational free equation

v13.629 defines the free control by deleting g while retaining
[
k_0(x,y)=-\lambda N_A(x,y).
]
On the full interval [-A,A], the even deficiency equation would require
[
\lambda (N_A q_e)(x)=\cosh x+B.
]
The Neumann inverse kernel is
[
N_A(x,y)=\frac{x^2+y^2}{4A}-\frac{|x-y|}{2}+\frac A6.
]

Direct differentiation gives
[
-\partial_x^2N_A(x,y)=\delta(x-y)-\frac1{2A},
]
and, crucially,
[
\partial_xN_A(\pm A,y)=0.
]
Thus every function in the range of N_A has Neumann endpoint derivative zero.

But
[
\frac d{dx}(\cosh x+B)|_{x=A}=\sinh A\ne0
]
for A>0. Therefore
[
\boxed{\lambda N_Aq_e=\cosh x+B}
]
has no continuum solution for any scalar B and finite nonzero lambda.

The odd equation has the same obstruction:
[
\lambda N_Aq_o=\sinh x+Cx,
]
whose endpoint derivative at x=A is
[
\cosh A+C.
]
One may choose C=-cosh A to repair the derivative at +A, but odd symmetry then fixes the corresponding condition at -A; the even sector already proves the full deficiency pair does not exist. In addition, the endpoint q conditions used in the Friedrichs construction are not generated by an independent free resolvent here.

## 2. Consequence for W_A^0

The finite Galerkin systems in v13.629 are solvable because projection onto
[
e_n(x)=\sqrt{2/A}\cos((n+1/2)\pi x/A),\qquad
o_n(x)=\sqrt{2/A}\sin((n+1)\pi x/A)
]
regularizes the incompatible continuum equation in finite dimension.

Hence the numerically computed W_{A,N,q}^0 is a finite-dimensional projected control. It does not converge to a separately defined exact continuum deficiency characteristic obtained by simply setting g=0.

Therefore an exact normalization constant for a continuum W_A^0 cannot be supplied: the object itself is not defined by the stated free deletion.

This corrects the stronger wording in v13.637/v13.640 that suggested an exact free characteristic could simply be derived and divided out.

## 3. Endpoint box factor

Although there is no exact continuum W_A^0, the endpoint-adapted finite spaces carry an exact geometric box scale. The observed free positive roots converge numerically to
[
r_n^0\to\frac{n\pi}{A},\qquad n=1,2,\ldots
]
(v13.629; independently reproduced in External Audit Round 67).

The entire function with precisely this symmetric nonzero zero set and a simple zero at z=0 is, up to a nonvanishing normalization,
[
E_A(z)=\sin(Az).
]
If the zero at the origin is to be removed, the canonical even endpoint factor is
[
\boxed{B_A(z)=\frac{\sin(Az)}{Az}},
qquad B_A(0)=1.
]
Its nonzero box-spectrum zeros are exactly
[
\boxed{z_n=\pm\frac{n\pi}{A},\quad n=1,2,\ldots.}
]

This factor is justified as the canonical entire function associated with the endpoint box zero set, NOT as an equality W_A^0=C_A sin(Az) for a solved continuum free deficiency problem.

## 4. Renormalization implication

A raw quotient W_full/W_free is not presently legitimate at continuum level because the denominator free deficiency problem is inconsistent.

The admissible next route is instead one of:
1. factor the canonical endpoint entire factor B_A(z)=sin(Az)/(Az) from a properly defined full characteristic and analyze the residual, proving that this factor is the correct asymptotic endpoint contribution; or
2. define a relative Fredholm determinant/resolvent against a genuinely well-posed reference operator sharing the same boundary/domain conditions.

Either route requires a derivation. Numerical division by the v13.629 projected free characteristic is only a finite-dimensional diagnostic and must not be promoted as the continuum renormalized object.
