## 2. Casimir/V4 thread: solid algebra, but the "unification" framing oversells what's actually shown

**[D] Verified by hand, and correct**, the core algebraic content across `v13.364`, `v13.365`, and `v13.368`: the mixed second difference of `F(u,v)=uv` on a lattice cell equals `δ²` (a re-derivation of the already-verified `v13.309` identity); the elementary completing-the-square identity `q(q+δ)=(q+δ/2)²-δ²/4`; the rank-one condition `F̂_{χ_0}F̂_{χ_{11}}=F̂_{χ_5}F̂_{χ_7}` (I checked this directly from the already-verified corner values: both sides equal `δ⁴(p+½)(q+½)/4` exactly); and the reciprocal-scaling degeneracy in `v13.368` (`AD=BC` for a general rectangle's Walsh data, trivial from commutativity, correctly used to show the aspect ratio is *not* recoverable from the four Walsh channels alone — a real, correctly-reasoned limitation result, not an overclaim).

**[I] However, the "Casimir/null-diamond/divisor-shell unification" framing in `v13.364` is weaker than it sounds, and I think this is exactly the thing worth watching that came up when we discussed this thread earlier.** The central claimed bridge is that the same quantity `M_F²=δ²/4` shows up in four places: the factor-cell mixed curvature, the null-diamond midpoint norm (asserted by citation to the earlier-audited Casimir/Null-Diamond foundation paper, not re-derived here), the `SU(2)` Casimir completion `T_c²=C+M_F²`, and the divisor-shell endpoint strip. I checked the Casimir piece specifically: `T_c²=C+¼` is just the standard identity `(j+½)²=j(j+1)+¼`, true for *any* angular momentum `j`, with no dependence on lattice spacing at all — the `¼` here is the constant from completing the square of a quadratic with unit linear coefficient, which coincides with `M_F²=δ²/4` only because `δ` was separately declared to equal `1` ("unit spacing... `C=T_c²-1/4`"). That's a naming coincidence at a specific parameter value, not a shared mechanism. What's actually being observed, underneath the vocabulary, is that the "quarter of the linear-coefficient-squared" correction term shows up whenever you either (a) take a mixed second difference of a bilinear product, or (b) complete the square of a quadratic — which are the same elementary algebraic move, and which will produce a `¼`-type constant in *any* context involving a product of two centered linear quantities, completely independent of whether that context is arithmetic (divisor shells), geometric (null diamonds), or operator-theoretic (`SU(2)` Casimirs). The entry's own guardrails ("equality of invariants does not imply causation," "no causal identification... is claimed") already concede this, which I credit — but the section headers and the word "unification" claim more than the guardrails allow, and I'd flag that gap explicitly rather than let the framing stand unqualified, especially since this is the specific direction the user asked to be pushed toward and specifically asked me to watch for exactly this failure mode.
