## 7. Exact screw–Weil pre-Hilbert isometry [D]

Let
\[
\mathcal D_W=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)
\]
(or any common dense test class with sufficient decay), and define the Weil sesquilinear form
\[
\langle F,H\rangle_W
=
\iint\mathscr W(u-v)F(u)\overline{H(v)}\,du\,dv.
\]

Let
\[
\mathcal D_S=D\mathcal D_W
=
\{F':F\in\mathcal D_W\},
\]
and define
\[
\langle f,h\rangle_S
=
\iint G_g(u,v)f(u)\overline{h(v)}\,du\,dv.
\]

Because every derivative has zero mass, the affine subtraction terms in \(G_g\) vanish. The integration-by-parts/Fourier identity gives the polarized equality
\[
\boxed{
\langle DF,DH\rangle_S
=
\langle F,H\rangle_W.
}
\]

Thus
\[
\boxed{
D:(\mathcal D_W,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_W)
\to
(\mathcal D_S,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_S)
}
\]
is an exact form isometry on the pre-Hilbert level whenever positivity is assumed.

On \(C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\), \(DF=0\) implies \(F=0\), so the derivative has no additional kernel there. On larger spaces the kernel is constants and must be quotiented:
\[
\boxed{\ker D=\operatorname{span}\{1\}.}

After quotienting the null spaces of the positive semidefinite forms and completing, the isometry extends uniquely to an isometry
\[
\boxed{

\]
Because \(\mathcal D_S\) was defined as \(D\mathcal D_W\), its range is dense by construction, hence the completed map is unitary onto \(\mathcal H_S\).

[G] This Hilbert completion is conditional on positivity of the forms; the algebraic/form identity is unconditional.

## 8. Zero-side carrier and unitary identification [C/D]

In the RH/real-zero specialization Suzuki's positive kernel has
\[

Define the screw zero carrier
\[
\boxed{
\Psi_u(\gamma)
=
\sqrt{m_\gamma}\,
\frac{e^{i\gamma u}-1}{\gamma}.
}
\]
Then
\[
G_g(u,v)
=
\langle\Psi_u,\Psi_v\rangle_{\ell^2(\Gamma)}.
\]

Differentiate:
\[
\partial_u\Psi_u(\gamma)
=
i\sqrt{m_\gamma}\,e^{i\gamma u}.
\]

Hence differentiation removes exactly the \(1/\gamma\) primitive weight. For a test function \(F\),
\[
\widehat{F'}(\gamma)=i\gamma\widehat F(\gamma),
\]
so
\[
\frac{|\widehat{F'}(\gamma)|^2}{\gamma^2}
=
|\widehat F(\gamma)|^2.
\]

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
Q_{\rm Suz}[F']
=
\sum_\gamma m_\gamma|\widehat F(\gamma)|^2
=
Q_{\rm Weil}[F].
}
\]

The phase \(i\) in the differentiated carrier is unit modulus and disappears in the Hermitian form. Thus, in the positive real-zero model, the derivative map is not merely equality of norms: after quotient/completion it is the unitary identification between the screw increment carrier and the Weil zero carrier.
