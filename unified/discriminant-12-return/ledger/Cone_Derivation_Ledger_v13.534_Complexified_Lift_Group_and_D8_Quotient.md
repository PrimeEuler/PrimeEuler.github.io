# Cone Derivation Ledger v13.534 — Complexified Arithmetic-Sign Lift Group and the D8 Quotient

## Status

Exact finite matrix-group calculation on the complexified cone. This refines v13.530. The natural order-four arithmetic-negation lift does **not** generate merely `D8` with the real cone reflections. The full generated group has order 16. Its central antipodal quotient is exactly `D8`.

No identification with Hamilton quaternions, split quaternions, or a Penrose/conformal model is asserted here; those remain interpretive side directions pending an explicit algebra map.

## 1. Generators

Use the affine-normalized `+i` lift from v13.530:

\[
A=\widetilde\sigma_+:
(X,Y,T)\mapsto(-T,iY,-X).
\]

Use the native factor-exchange reflection

\[
B=R_X:(X,Y,T)\mapsto(-X,Y,T).
\]

Then

\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&-1\\
0&i&0\\
-1&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The square of the arithmetic-sign lift is

\[
A^2=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&-1&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}
=R_Y.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{A^2=R_Y,\qquad A^4=I.}
\]

Thus `R_Y` need not be added independently:

\[
\langle R_X,R_Y,\widetilde\sigma_+\rangle
=\langle B,A\rangle.
\]

## 2. The conjugation relation is not dihedral

Direct multiplication gives

\[
BAB\neq A^{-1}.
\]

Define

\[
H=R_XS_T
=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,-1).
\]

The commutator calculation gives

\[
\boxed{[B,A]=H,}
\]

with

\[
H^2=I,
\qquad [H,A]=[H,B]=I.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{BAB=AH.}
\]

Hence the group generated by the natural complexified lift and `R_X` is **not** the ordinary dihedral group of order eight.

## 3. Exact order and presentation

The commuting subgroup

\[
N=\langle A,H\rangle
\]

has

\[
A^4=H^2=1,
\qquad [A,H]=1,
\]

so

\[
N\cong C_4\times C_2.
\]

Conjugation by `B` fixes `H` and sends

\[
A\mapsto AH.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
G_{\rm lift}
=\langle A,B\rangle
\cong(C_4\times C_2)\rtimes C_2,
}
\]

where the final `C2` acts by

\[
A\mapsto AH,
\qquad H\mapsto H.
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{|G_{\rm lift}|=16.}
\]

An exact presentation is

\[
\boxed{
G_{\rm lift}
=\langle A,H,B\mid
A^4=H^2=B^2=1,
[A,H]=[B,H]=1,
BAB=AH
\rangle.
}
\]

The element-order census is

\[
\boxed{
1\text{ identity},\qquad
7\text{ involutions},\qquad
8\text{ elements of order }4.
}
\]

## 4. Center and the full antipode

The center has order four:

\[
Z(G_{\rm lift})\cong C_2^2.
\]

It is generated by

\[
A^2=R_Y
\]

and

\[
H=R_XS_T.
\]

Their product is

\[
A^2H
=R_YR_XS_T
=R_XR_YS_T
=-I_3.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{-I_3:(X,Y,T)\mapsto(-X,-Y,-T)}
\]

is a central element of the lift group.

Explicitly,

\[
\boxed{
Z(G_{\rm lift})
=\{I,R_Y,R_XS_T,-I_3\}.
}
\]

This is exactly the full cone antipode previously appearing as the candidate image of the QR deck direction under the chosen real `C2^3` intertwiner.

That coincidence is recorded but not promoted to a canonical QR identification here.

## 5. The dihedral quotient

The key relation is

\[
H(-I_3)=R_Y,
\]

or equivalently

\[
H\equiv R_Y=A^2\pmod{\langle-I_3\rangle}.
\]

Therefore in the quotient

\[
\overline G
=G_{\rm lift}/\langle-I_3\rangle,
\]

the conjugation law becomes

\[
\bar B\bar A\bar B
=\bar A\bar H
=\bar A\bar A^2
=\bar A^3
=\bar A^{-1}.
\]

Together with

\[
\bar A^4=1,
\qquad \bar B^2=1,
\]

this is the standard dihedral presentation. Hence

\[
\boxed{
G_{\rm lift}/\langle-I_3\rangle\cong D_8.
}
\]

Thus the natural complexified arithmetic-sign geometry supplies a central two-fold cover

\[
\boxed{
1\longrightarrow C_2
\longrightarrow G_{\rm lift}
\longrightarrow D_8
\longrightarrow1,
}
\]

where the kernel is generated by the full antipode `-I_3`.

## 6. Comparison with the intrinsic complex-plane D8

On the fixed-`T` complex cone circle, define

\[
J:z\mapsto iz,
\qquad C:z\mapsto\bar z.
\]

Then

\[
J^4=C^2=1,
\qquad CJC=J^{-1},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\langle J,C\rangle\cong D_8.}
\]

The complexified signed-factor lift gives a second construction whose **quotient** has the same presentation:

\[
\bar A^4=\bar B^2=1,
\qquad
\bar B\bar A\bar B=\bar A^{-1}.
\]

Therefore there is an abstract isomorphism

\[
\boxed{
G_{\rm lift}/\langle-I_3\rangle
\cong
\langle J,C\rangle
\cong D_8.
}
\]

At this checkpoint this is an exact abstract group isomorphism. An explicit operator intertwiner between the quotient action and the fixed-`T` complex-plane action remains to be constructed.

The earlier `A3` standard representation identified the `chi_12` axis as the axis fixed by the `11` half-turn. Its stabilizer inside the tetrahedral `S4` is a dihedral subgroup of order eight.

Thus three independently arising order-eight structures are now in view:

2. the central-antipodal quotient `G_lift/<-I_3> ≅ D8`;

## 8. Structural interpretation

The order-16 result explains why the naive expectation `\langle R_X,R_Y,\widetilde\sigma\rangle=D8` fails. The complexified arithmetic-sign lift carries one additional central sign: the full antipode.

Projectivizing the cone removes precisely this central antipode. Consequently the projective complexified cone sees the ordinary dihedral group:

\[
\boxed{
\text{complexified affine lift group }G_{\rm lift}
\xrightarrow{/\{\pm I\}}
D_8.
}
\]

This makes the projective-null-cone/Penrose-style viewpoint mathematically relevant: the arithmetic sign action becomes dihedral only after antipodal projectivization.

This is an exact projective-group observation; no Penrose conformal-compactification theorem is asserted.

## 9. Quaternion / split-quaternion guardrail

The real cone

\[
X^2+Y^2-T^2=0
\]

is a null cone for an indefinite quadratic form, so a split-quaternion / real Clifford-algebra model is structurally more natural than the positive-definite Hamilton quaternion norm.

However, the exact group calculated above is **not** identified here with `Q8`. The projective quotient is dihedral:

\[
\boxed{D_8,\text{ not }Q_8.}
\]

Any split-quaternion interpretation must reproduce the explicit matrices and central extension above before being used as more than an explanatory analogy.
