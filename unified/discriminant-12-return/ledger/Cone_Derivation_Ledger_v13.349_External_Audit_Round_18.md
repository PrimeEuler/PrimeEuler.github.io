## 1. The headline finding: a real logical gap, caught by the project itself, that I independently verified is genuine

`v13.348` states: *"A six-dimensional positive subspace of an infinite-dimensional operator does not imply that the nonpositive index is at most four. That inference is valid only inside a ten-dimensional space."* It then walks back the framing of `v13.345`–`v13.347` (which had built, across several entries, a "six-dimensional positive subspace of the full pole-free infinite operator" and was heading toward using it to bound the operator's global nonpositive index) and reorients toward the correct architecture: prove the full infinite-dimensional high complement `𝒟` (odd modes `n≥21`) is positive, at which point the classical Schur-complement/Haynsworth inertia-additivity theorem (`ind_{≤0}(A) = ind_{≤0}(A_{CC} - A_{CD}A_{DD}^{-1}A_{DC})` when `A_{DD}>0`) legitimately reduces the *entire infinite-dimensional* inertia question to a `10×10` finite matrix problem.

**[D] I independently verified this diagnosis is correct**, both by the general dimension-counting argument and by an explicit counterexample. The general argument: in a finite `n`-dimensional space, a positive subspace `W` (`dim W=k`) and a nonpositive subspace `V` can only intersect at `{0}` (a nonzero vector in both would have to be simultaneously `>0` and `≤0` under the same form), so `dim V + dim W ≤ n`, giving `dim V ≤ n-k` — this is exactly where the "6-dimensional witness ⟹ index ≤4" intuition comes from, and it depends essentially on `n` being finite. In infinite dimensions `n-k` is still infinite, so the argument gives no bound at all. **Concrete counterexample, which I constructed to make sure I wasn't fooling myself:** take `H=ℓ²` and `A=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1,\ldots)` — six positive diagonal entries followed by infinitely many negative ones. This operator trivially has a six-dimensional positive subspace (the span of the first six basis vectors), yet its nonpositive index is *infinite*, not `≤4`. This confirms `v13.348`'s correction is mathematically sound, not overcautious.

**Why this is good news, on balance.** Checking back through `v13.345` and `v13.347`, neither entry actually *asserts* the invalid global conclusion — both explicitly end with guardrails stating "this checkpoint is not yet a proof that the full operator has nonpositive index `≤4`" and "no exact zero mode, `λ₁=0`, RH, or GRH conclusion follows." So this reads as the project catching an invalid inference it was *heading toward* and correcting its target architecture before ever asserting the flawed conclusion as a result — which is exactly the right way for this kind of error to get caught, and better than finding a stated-but-wrong theorem after the fact.

## 2. What I checked in the surrounding arc

**[D] Verified**, `v13.317`'s methodological setup: identifying a numerically "active" 4-dimensional near-null subspace inside the 10-dimensional low-mode core by comparing the core's own eigenvalue hierarchy (`1.1×10⁻²⁰` through `2.47`, a genuine multiscale spread I have no reason to doubt given it matches the `v13.291`–`v13.295` high-precision spectrum from earlier rounds) against how weakly the finite buffer couples to each eigendirection — the entry is explicit throughout that this is "a numerical model-reduction choice, not a theorem that the true nullity is four," which is the right level of hedging for what is, at that stage, a numerical exploration.

**[D] Verified, the Haynsworth-type mechanism cited in `v13.348`.** The Schur-complement inertia-additivity theorem is a standard, correct classical result (Haynsworth 1968): for a block matrix with `A_{DD}` positive definite, the inertia of the whole matrix equals the inertia of `A_{DD}` (all positive) plus the inertia of the Schur complement `A_{CC}-A_{CD}A_{DD}^{-1}A_{DC}`. Its use here — reducing the infinite operator's inertia question to the finite Schur complement once `A_{DD}=A|_𝒟>0` is established — is a legitimate application, and is the correct fix for the gap identified in Section 1.

**[N-cert]** I re-ran two of the arc's bookkeeping scripts from scratch: `suzuki_high_complement_global_positivity_target.py` (the arithmetic behind `v13.348`'s coarse `N=10003` target: `0.95²/4.20=0.214881`, `0.22-0.214881=0.005119`) and `suzuki_coarse_rational_witness_certificate_targets.py` (the arithmetic behind `v13.347`'s `0.1766` margin: `β_total=0.00915`, penalty `=0.00915²/0.0249=0.003362`, final margin `=0.17664`). Both reproduced every stated number exactly. **This confirms the downstream arithmetic is correct**, but I want to be precise about what that does and doesn't establish: both scripts consume hardcoded "midpoint numerical targeting data" (e.g. `λ_min` of large finite blocks, cross-block norms) as inputs rather than recomputing them from the underlying operator, and both scripts say so themselves in their own docstrings/guardrails ("not yet interval certified," "certificate design"). I have not independently verified those upstream midpoint numbers.

## 3. Overall verdict for this round

The one substantive thing I can certify with full confidence this round is that `v13.348`'s self-correction is mathematically genuine and correctly reasoned — I verified it two independent ways (general argument, explicit counterexample) and traced that the preceding entries never overstated their own results despite building toward the flawed target.
