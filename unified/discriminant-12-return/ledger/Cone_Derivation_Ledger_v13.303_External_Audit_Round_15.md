## 4. v13.300→v13.301: a real mathematical error caught and fixed — independently confirmed on both sides

**[Audit] This is the most substantively interesting catch in the batch, and I confirm both halves of it.** `v13.300` observed that fixed-size finite compressions of the cusp residual `C-D_log` shrank as the cutoff moved to higher modes, and tentatively read this as evidence of compactness. `v13.301` correctly identifies this as a classic finite-window fallacy: shrinking *entries* along a fixed-size moving window is not the same as compactness of the operator, and `v13.301` exhibits the actual obstruction explicitly — `C-D_log` contains **one-half of the classical Hilbert matrix**, `(H_odd)_{mn}=1/(m+n)`, which is bounded (norm `π/2` by the century-old Hilbert-inequality/Hilbert-matrix norm theorem, correctly cited) but **not compact**. This is a genuine, nontrivial, correctly-diagnosed error in the immediately preceding entry, caught by the project itself via an exact calculation rather than more numerics. **[D] Verified by hand, the entire exact closed-form derivation**, independently re-derived and checked by direct high-precision numerical integration (not reusing the project's code):
- the simplified self-overlap `S_{mn}(t)=\frac{2ab}{a^2-b^2}[b\sin(bt)-a\sin(at)]` for `a=mπ/2,b=nπ/2` — confirmed exactly against the raw definition `C_{mn}(t)+C_{nm}(t)` for several `(m,n,t)` triples (agreement to ~30 digits);
- the off-diagonal closed form `C_{mn}=\frac2\pi\frac{n\,\mathrm{Si}(m\pi)-m\,\mathrm{Si}(n\pi)}{m^2-n^2}` — confirmed exactly by direct numerical integration of `∫_0^2 s(t)S_{mn}(t)dt` against the closed form for `(m,n)=(1,3),(3,5),(9,11),(31,33),(31,41)`, matching both the closed form and the ledger's own quoted representative values (e.g. `C_{31,41}≈-0.0140483`) exactly;
- the diagonal closed form `C_{nn}=\log(n/4)-\mathrm{Ci}(n\pi)-\mathrm{Si}(n\pi)/(n\pi)` — confirmed exactly the same way for `n=1,3,5,11,13,31`, again matching the ledger's own earlier `v13.298` diagonal values (e.g. `n=31: 2.03135`) exactly;
- the extraction of the Hilbert term via `Si(jπ)=π/2+δ_j`: substituting the constant `π/2` into the off-diagonal formula gives exactly `-1/(m+n)`, which I re-derived independently and matches the boxed claim.

This is genuinely elegant, fully exact mathematics (not an approximation or a fit), and it is completely correct.

## 5. v13.302: the Hilbert-Schmidt "proof" — mostly correct, with one real gap

**[D] Verified by hand, and correct.** The asymptotic expansions `Si(nπ)=π/2+1/(nπ)+δ(nπ)` with `|δ(x)|≤2/x³+6/x⁴`, and `Ci(nπ)=1/(nπ)²+ρ(nπ)` with `|ρ(x)|≤2/x³`, both follow from repeated integration by parts of the standard tail integrals `π/2-\mathrm{Si}(x)=\int_x^\infty(\sin t)/t\,dt` and `\mathrm{Ci}(x)=-\int_x^\infty(\cos t)/t\,dt`, using `\cos(n\pi)=-1,\sin(n\pi)=0` at each step to kill boundary terms. I re-derived both bounds independently from scratch via the same elementary IBP-and-triangle-inequality route and got exactly the stated constants.

**[D] Verified by hand, and correct**, the diagonal Hilbert-Schmidt argument (`v13.302` §5): substituting these expansions into `K_{nn}=-\mathrm{Ci}(n\pi)-\mathrm{Si}(n\pi)/(n\pi)+1/(2n)`, the `π/(2n\pi)=1/(2n)` terms cancel exactly as claimed, leaving `|K_{nn}|\le 2/x^2+2/x^3+2/x^4+6/x^5` with `x=n\pi` — I re-derived this bound term-by-term and it is exactly right, and `Σ|K_{nn}|²<∞` follows immediately since `K_{nn}=O(n^{-2})`.

**[Audit] The off-diagonal square-summability argument (§3) does not establish what it claims, though the conclusion is very likely still true.** The entry bounds `|E_{mn}|\le\frac1\pi\frac{nd_m+md_n}{m+n}` (correct algebra, I re-checked it) and then asserts this "recovers an absolutely convergent square-sum" after "splitting the lattice into `m≤n` and `n<m`." I tested this directly: for fixed `m` and `n\to\infty`, the term `nd_m/(m+n)\to d_m`, a **nonzero constant** — it does not decay in `n` at all. I confirmed this numerically (fixing `m=5` and evaluating both the exact `E_{mn}` and the stated bound at `n=7,9,21,...,2001`): the exact `|E_{mn}|` correctly decays like `C/n` (as it must — I verified `|E_{mn}|\cdot n` stabilizes near a constant `≈3×10⁻⁴`), but the *stated bound* itself does **not** decay — it stabilizes near `1.95×10⁻⁴` and stays there as `n\to\infty`. A bound that doesn't tend to zero cannot be summed to a finite value over the infinitely many `n` for a fixed `m`, so as written, this estimate cannot be the basis for the square-summability conclusion the entry draws from it. I then directly computed `Σ_{m,n\text{ odd},\,m\ne n}E_{mn}^2` over growing finite windows using the *exact* formula (not the bound) and found it does converge nicely (`0.000269, 0.000281, 0.000288, 0.000291` for lattices of half-width `10,20,40,80`) — so **the claim itself (`K_cusp` is Hilbert-Schmidt) is almost certainly true and is independently supported by both this direct computation and `v13.301`'s own Frobenius-norm stabilization near `0.2085`**. What's missing is a correct proof of that specific piece: the entry needs a sharper joint estimate on `E_{mn}` (e.g., separately bounding the `n\gg m` and `m\gg n` regimes using the actual `O(\min(m,n)^{-3}\max(m,n)^{-1})`-type decay I observed numerically, rather than the crude triangle-inequality bound that was used) before "`K_cusp\in\mathcal S_2`" can be called a completed proof rather than a well-evidenced conjecture with one gap in its supporting argument.

## 8. Guardrails

All guardrails from prior rounds remain in force. **New guardrail, prompted by Section 5 above:**

- **A bound used to establish square-summability (or any convergence claim) over an infinite two-index lattice must itself be checked to decay to zero along every path to infinity in the index pair — not just be "small" at the specific points where it was first evaluated.** Concretely: before asserting `Σf(m,n)²<∞` from a stated bound `|f(m,n)|\le B(m,n)`, evaluate `B(m,n)` for fixed `m` as `n\to\infty` (and vice versa) and confirm it actually tends to zero; a bound that stabilizes at a nonzero constant along such a path cannot be summed over that path, regardless of how small the constant is. This is exactly the gap in `v13.302`'s off-diagonal estimate, and it is a natural companion to the round-14 guardrail about validating near-cancellation diagnostics — both are about not mistaking "small at the tested points" for "actually decaying where the argument needs it to."
