## 1. What Suzuki actually conjectures

Under RH, Suzuki identifies the infinite Hilbert-Pólya operator
\[
D=D_{\pi/2}
\]
as the self-adjoint extension of the closed symmetric infinite operator generated by translations.

He then states that, since finite D_a acts as i d/dx on compactly supported functions, it is natural to expect that BY CHOOSING
\[
\boxed{\theta=\theta(a)\ \text{appropriately}}
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
D_{a,\theta(a)}\to D
}
\]
in strong resolvent sense as a->infinity.

Thus the conjecture controls one a-dependent self-adjoint extension, not the whole finite theta-family and not a fixed ordered pair such as (theta=pi,theta=0).

## 2. What Section 7.6 supplies

Suzuki embeds
\[
\boxed{
H(A_a)\hookrightarrow H(A_\infty)
}
\]
injectively. Under this embedding,
\[
D_a=D_\infty|_{H(A_a)}
\]
and the finite D_{a,theta} can be regarded as operators on H(A_infty), though they are no longer densely defined there.

The source then says it is natural to expect D_{a,theta} to approximate D_{pi/2}=D and its eigenvalues to approximate the zero ordinates Gamma.

It does NOT state:
- convergence for every fixed theta;
- simultaneous convergence of theta=0 and theta=pi;
- convergence of finite boundary triples;
- convergence of gamma fields;
- convergence of Weyl functions;
- convergence of finite de Branges kernels.

## 3. Consequence of v13.682

v13.682 proved abstractly that convergence of a single reference extension cannot determine the Weyl function because
\[
\Gamma_1\mapsto\Gamma_1+r\Gamma_0
\]
leaves ker Gamma_0 unchanged but sends
\[
m(z)\mapsto m(z)+r.
\]

Suzuki's conjecture is weaker still for our purpose because the convergent extension itself may have a-dependent phase theta(a).

Therefore:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Suzuki's strong-resolvent conjecture alone does not imply }
m_a\to m_\infty.
}
\]

Nor does it directly imply convergence of the project ratio
\[
R_a^{proj}
=
\frac{W_\pi}{W_\pi-cW_0}.
\]

## 4. Moving-phase interpretation

A moving self-adjoint phase is equivalent to a moving real boundary parameter
\[
\tau_a=\tan(\theta(a)/2)
\]
in the project boundary coordinates (subject to the fixed convention map).

The rank-one resolvent depends on
\[
\tau_a-m_a(z).
\]
Strong resolvent convergence of D_{a,theta(a)} can therefore constrain this COMBINATION while leaving m_a itself underdetermined unless tau_a is independently controlled.

This exposes the exact missing datum:
\[
\boxed{
\text{one must control the boundary phase }\theta(a)
\text{ (or }\tau_a\text{) in the limit.}
}
\]

## 5. Minimal project strengthening

There are two clean routes.

### Route A: fixed-pair convergence
Prove compatible generalized strong-resolvent convergence of two distinct fixed finite extensions, for example
\[
D_{a,\pi}\to D_{\infty,\alpha},
\qquad
D_{a,0}\to D_{\infty,\beta},
\qquad
\alpha\ne\beta.
\]
The ordered pair fixes the scalar boundary coordinate and removes the additive Weyl ambiguity.

### Route B: one moving extension plus calibrated phase
Prove
\[
D_{a,\theta(a)}\to D
\]
together with
\[
\tau_a\to\tau_\infty
\]
in a boundary coordinate that itself converges, plus convergence of one normalized defect vector. Then the Krein denominator
\[
\tau_a-m_a(z)
\]
and the known tau_a can recover m_a.

Route B is closer to Suzuki's actual conjecture; Route A is cleaner for the project's finite de Branges pair.

## 6. Important phase mismatch

Suzuki's infinite distinguished translation extension is labelled
\[
\boxed{\psi=\pi/2}
\]
in the infinite de Branges family M_psi / D_psi.

The project's finite HB pair currently uses theta=0 and theta=pi.

These labels belong to separately normalized finite and infinite deficiency bases. They MUST NOT be identified numerically without deriving the phase transport between the bases.

Therefore a future statement such as "theta(a)->pi/2" is unsupported until the finite-to-infinite deficiency-basis phase is fixed.

## 7. Relation to Corollary 1.6

Corollary 1.6 itself allows BOTH
\[
\theta=\theta(a)
\]
and an entire normalization
\[
e^{\phi(a,z)}.
\]
Suzuki even remarks that theta=pi may be natural, but explicitly does not pursue the choice.

Hence the source leaves two independent asymptotic freedoms:
1. extension phase theta(a);
2. entire characteristic gauge phi(a,z).

The project boundary-triple program has now separated these precisely:
- theta(a) controls the real boundary coordinate / Weyl origin;
- phi(a,z) controls the common entire gauge.
