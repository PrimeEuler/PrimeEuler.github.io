# Cone Derivation Ledger v13.487 — A3 Root and Weight Lattices in Character Coordinates

## Scope

Continue v13.485 by determining the root lattice Q(A3) and weight lattice P(A3) inside the character-coordinate space
\[
W=\mathbb R^3=\operatorname{span}\{\chi_{-4},\chi_{-3},\chi_{12}\},
\]
and locating the four tetrahedral character vertices exactly relative to these lattices.

The main refinement is that the primitive A3 roots are half the differences of the original character-sign vertices. Consequently the original vertices themselves are not roots or fundamental weights: they are twice the minuscule tetrahedral weight orbit.

---

## 1. Simple roots

Use the v13.485 simple roots
\[
\alpha_1=(0,1,1),\qquad
\alpha_2=(1,-1,0),\qquad
\alpha_3=(0,1,-1).
\]
They have Cartan matrix
\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
All roots are
\[
\Phi=\{(\pm1,\pm1,0),(\pm1,0,\pm1),(0,\pm1,\pm1)\}.
\]

---

## 2. Root lattice

The root lattice is
\[
Q=\mathbb Z\alpha_1+\mathbb Z\alpha_2+\mathbb Z\alpha_3.
\]
Every root has even coordinate sum, so
\[
Q\subseteq\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb Z^3:x+y+z\equiv0\pmod2\}.
\]
Conversely the simple-root matrix has determinant 2, so Q has index 2 in \(\mathbb Z^3\). The even-sum sublattice also has index 2. Hence
\[
\boxed{Q(A_3)=D_3=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb Z^3:x+y+z\equiv0\pmod2\}.}
\]
Thus
\[
[\mathbb Z^3:Q]=2.
\]

---

## 3. Fundamental weights

Because \(\alpha_i^2=2\), the fundamental weights \(\omega_i\) are determined by
\[
\omega_i\cdot\alpha_j=\delta_{ij}.
\]
Solving gives
\[
\boxed{\omega_1=\left(\frac12,\frac12,\frac12\right),}
\]
\[
\boxed{\omega_2=(1,0,0),}
\]
\[
\boxed{\omega_3=\left(\frac12,\frac12,-\frac12\right).}
\]
Their norms are
\[
\omega_1^2=\omega_3^2=\frac34,\qquad \omega_2^2=1,
\]
and their Gram matrix is \(C^{-1}\):
\[
(\omega_i\cdot\omega_j)=
\frac14\begin{pmatrix}3&2&1\\2&4&2\\1&2&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]

---

## 4. Weight lattice

The weight lattice is the dual lattice of Q:
\[
P=Q^*=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:x\cdot Q\subseteq\mathbb Z\}.
\]
Since Q is generated by vectors \(\pm e_i\pm e_j\), the conditions are
\[
x_i+x_j\in\mathbb Z,\qquad x_i-x_j\in\mathbb Z
\]
for every pair i,j. Therefore all three coordinates have the same fractional part, and that fractional part is either 0 or 1/2. Hence
\[
\boxed{P(A_3)=\mathbb Z^3\ \cup\ \left(\mathbb Z+\frac12\right)^3.}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
P=\mathbb Z\omega_1+\mathbb Z\omega_2+\mathbb Z\omega_3.
\]

We have the exact lattice chain
\[
\boxed{Q\subset\mathbb Z^3\subset P,}
\]
with
\[
[\mathbb Z^3:Q]=2,\qquad[P:\mathbb Z^3]=2,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{[P:Q]=4.}
\]
As expected for A3,
\[
\boxed{P/Q\cong\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z.}
\]

## 5. The four original character vertices

The v13.485 vertices are
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
v_1&=(1,1,1),\\
v_5&=(1,-1,-1),\\
v_7&=(-1,1,-1),\\
v_{11}&=(-1,-1,1).
\end{aligned}}
\]
Each lies in \(\mathbb Z^3\subset P\). But every one has odd coordinate sum, so
\[
\boxed{v_r\notin Q.}
\]
Thus the four vertices occupy the nontrivial coset
\[
\boxed{(1,1,1)+Q=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb Z^3:x+y+z\equiv1\pmod2\}.}
\]
Their pairwise differences lie in 2Q:
\[
v_r-v_s=2\alpha_{r,s}.
\]
This is exactly why v13.485 divided edge differences by 2 to obtain primitive roots.

## 6. The true minuscule tetrahedral weight orbit

The key lattice refinement is obtained by halving the character vertices:
\[
\mu_r=\frac12v_r.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mu_1&=(1/2,1/2,1/2)=\omega_1,\\
\mu_5&=(1/2,-1/2,-1/2),\\
\mu_7&=(-1/2,1/2,-1/2),\\
\mu_{11}&=(-1/2,-1/2,1/2).
\end{aligned}}
\]
These four points lie in \(P\setminus\mathbb Z^3\), and they form the Weyl orbit
\[
\boxed{W(A_3)\cdot\omega_1=\{\mu_1,\mu_5,\mu_7,\mu_{11}\}.}
\]
They are the four weights of the minuscule fundamental representation of highest weight \(\omega_1\) (up to the chosen A3 orientation).

Most importantly,
\[
\boxed{\mu_r-\mu_s=\alpha_{r,s}\in\Phi.}
\]
So at the natural weight-lattice scale, the tetrahedron's six edge differences are the six positive/negative root pairs directly, with no extra factor.

The original character vertices are therefore
\[
\boxed{v_r=2\mu_r.}
\]
They are a dilation by 2 of the minuscule weight tetrahedron.

## 7. Their class in P/Q

Since
\[
v_1=2\omega_1,
\]
and all four vertices differ by elements of Q after division? More precisely, \(v_r-v_s=2\alpha_{r,s}\in Q\), so all v_r define the same class in P/Q:
\[
\boxed{[v_r]=[2\omega_1]\in P/Q.}
\]
The class \([\omega_1]\) generates
\[
P/Q\cong\mathbb Z/4,
\]
so \([2\omega_1]\) is its unique element of order 2.

Thus the original four character-sign vertices all lie in the unique order-two coset of the A3 discriminant group:
\[
\boxed{v_r\in 2\omega_1+Q.}
\]

By contrast, the halved vertices \(\mu_r\) lie in the generator coset
\[
\boxed{\mu_r\in\omega_1+Q.}
\]
(up to replacing the generator by its inverse under the opposite orientation).

## 8. Four cosets of P/Q in these coordinates

A convenient set of representatives is
\[
0,\quad \omega_1,\quad 2\omega_1,\quad 3\omega_1.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
P=Q\sqcup(\omega_1+Q)\sqcup(2\omega_1+Q)\sqcup(3\omega_1+Q).}
\]
Geometrically:

- Q is the even-sum integer lattice;
- \(2\omega_1+Q\) is the odd-sum integer lattice;
- the two remaining cosets are the two parity classes of half-integer points.

The original character-coordinate lattice \(\mathbb Z^3\) is exactly the union of the first and order-two cosets:
\[
\boxed{\mathbb Z^3=Q\sqcup(2\omega_1+Q).}
\]
The full weight lattice adds the two half-integral cosets:
\[
\boxed{P\setminus\mathbb Z^3=(\omega_1+Q)\sqcup(3\omega_1+Q).}
\]

## 9. Scale hierarchy

There are now three natural scales in character coordinates:

### Character-sign scale
\[
v_r\in\mathbb Z^3,
\qquad \|v_r\|^2=3,
\]
with edge squared length 8.

### Weight scale
\[
\mu_r=v_r/2\in P,
\qquad \|\mu_r\|^2=3/4,
\]
with edge squared length 2.

### Root scale
\[
\alpha_{r,s}=\mu_r-\mu_s\in Q,
\qquad \|\alpha_{r,s}\|^2=2.
\]

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\text{character tetrahedron}=2\times\text{minuscule A3 weight tetrahedron},
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\text{root}=\text{difference of two minuscule tetrahedral weights}.
}
\]

## 10. Compatibility with the same S4 matrices

All v13.483 signed-permutation matrices have integral entries, preserve Q, and are orthogonal. Hence they also preserve the dual lattice P:
\[
M_gQ=Q,\qquad M_gP=P.
\]
They permute the original vertices v_r and therefore also their halves \(\mu_r\):
\[
M_g\mu_r=\mu_{g(r)}.
\]
Consequently
\[
M_g(\mu_r-\mu_s)=\mu_{g(r)}-\mu_{g(s)},
\]
so the same matrices simultaneously act on
\[
\boxed{
Q\subset\mathbb Z^3\subset P,
}
\]
the root system \(\Phi\), the minuscule weight tetrahedron, and the doubled character-sign tetrahedron.

## 11. Structural conclusion

The discriminant-12 character coordinates do more than exhibit an abstract A3 root system. They place the relevant objects at precise lattice levels:

\[
\boxed{
Q(A_3)=\{\text{even-sum integer triples}\},
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
P(A_3)=\mathbb Z^3\cup(\mathbb Z+1/2)^3,
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
P/Q\cong\mathbb Z/4.
}
\]

The four raw character-sign states are
\[
\boxed{v_r\in2\omega_1+Q,}
\]
the unique order-two coset, while their halves are
\[
\boxed{\mu_r=v_r/2\in\omega_1+Q,}
\]
the minuscule four-weight orbit. Their pairwise differences are exactly the twelve oriented A3 roots.

Thus the precise lattice chain is
\[
\boxed{
\text{discriminant-12 character signs }v_r
\xrightarrow{/2}
\text{minuscule weights }\mu_r
\xrightarrow{\text{differences}}
A_3\text{ roots }\alpha_{r,s}.
}
\]

Guardrail: this is an exact lattice/representation statement in the Hadamard character-coordinate space. It does not by itself identify these weights with geometric cone-shell vectors or resolve the separate prime-2 residue/tangent semilinear-frame question.
