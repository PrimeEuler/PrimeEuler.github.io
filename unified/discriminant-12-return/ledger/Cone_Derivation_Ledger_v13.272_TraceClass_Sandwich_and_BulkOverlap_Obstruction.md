## 5. Exact compressed-free model

To see what a naive bulk translation model actually produces, take the full-line free positive operator

\[
A_{\rm free}=-\frac{d^2}{dx^2}
\]

with resolvent parameter

\[
w=\kappa^2,
\qquad \kappa>0.
\]

Its full-line resolvent kernel is

\[
\boxed{
G_\kappa(x,y)
=
\frac1{2\kappa}e^{-\kappa|x-y|}.
}
\]

Compress it to `I_a`:

\[
R_{a,\kappa}:=P_a(A_{\rm free}+\kappa^2)^{-1}P_a.
\]

Now take the truncated shift `S_{a,v}` with `0\le v\le2a`.

The trace of `R_{a,\kappa}S_{a,v}` is computed directly from the kernel.

The shift contributes only where both `x` and `x-v` lie in `I_a`, an interval of length

\[
2a-v.
\]

On that overlap,

\[
|x-(x-v)|=v.
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}(R_{a,\kappa}S_{a,v})
&=
\int_{I_a\cap(I_a+v)}
G_\kappa(x,x-v)\,dx
\\
&=
\int_{I_a\cap(I_a+v)}
\frac1{2\kappa}e^{-\kappa v}\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(R_{a,\kappa}S_{a,v})
=
\frac{2a-v}{2\kappa}e^{-\kappa v}.
}
\]

By symmetry,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}
\bigl(R_{a,\kappa}(S_{a,v}+S_{a,v}^*)\bigr)
=
\frac{2a-v}{\kappa}e^{-\kappa v}.
}
\]

This is exact for the compressed full-line model.

## 7. Bulk-overlap obstruction

The previous calculation proves the following model no-go statement.

### Theorem — naive bulk-shift mismatch

For the compressed free one-dimensional resolvent on `[-a,a]`, an unnormalized truncated shift at distance `v` contributes to the relative trace with multiplicity equal to the overlap length `2a-v`.

Therefore a perturbation with the natural arithmetic coefficient

\[
b_K(n)n^{-1/2}
\]

cannot reproduce the D12 finite Euler trace term

\[
-\frac1{\sqrt w}
\frac{b_K(n)}{n^{1/2+\sqrt w}}
\]

by a plain bulk translation trace.

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\text{naive bulk translation model}
\neq
\text{D12 Euler spectral shift target}.
}
\]

No asymptotic limit in `a` fixes this term-by-term: for fixed `n`, the unwanted factor behaves like `2a`.

Thus the arithmetic target is not a volume-density trace of a fixed local translation perturbation unless one explicitly divides out the overlap volume.

## 8. Three possible repairs

The obstruction is informative because it leaves only a small number of natural mechanisms.

### 8.1 Overlap-normalized shifts

Define

\[
\widehat S_{a,v}
:=
\frac{1}{2a-v}S_{a,v}.
\]

Then in the compressed-free model,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(R_{a,\kappa}\widehat S_{a,v})
=
\frac1{2\kappa}e^{-\kappa v}.
}
\]

The overlap factor is removed exactly.

However, the current screw-form derivation does not yet supply such a normalization, so introducing it by hand would be ad hoc.

### 8.2 Boundary trace rather than bulk trace

The Euler explicit formula counts a prime power once, not once for every translate inside the interval.

This suggests that the correct spectral observable may be a boundary/Weyl trace or a relative determinant boundary phase rather than the Hilbert-space bulk trace.

This possibility is compatible with v13.264–v13.268, where boundary functionals and finite-dimensional Krein determinants repeatedly emerged as the natural objects.

### 8.3 Nontrivial screw metric cancels the overlap

The localized Suzuki operator is not simply the free Laplacian plus raw shifts on ordinary `L^2(I_a)`.

Its Hilbert structure/form kernel is built from the screw function itself.

Therefore the relevant trace density may contain a compensating factor coming from the form metric, Friedrichs map, or nonlocal conjugation.

This is the most structurally plausible repair, but it must be derived rather than assumed.
