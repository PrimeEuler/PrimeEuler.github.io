# Cone Derivation Ledger v13.485 — Tetrahedral Character Vertices and the A3 Root System

## Scope

Continue v13.483 by constructing the four tetrahedral vertices in the nontrivial Hadamard character coordinates
\[
W=\operatorname{span}\{\chi_{-4},\chi_{-3},\chi_{12}\}\cong\mathbb R^3,
\]
forming all six unoriented edge differences (twelve oriented roots), and verifying that they form a root system of type \(A_3\) preserved by the same signed-permutation \(S_4\) matrices.

All calculations are exact.

---

## 1. Four character vertices

For \(r\in U(12)=\{1,5,7,11\}\), define its character-sign vector
\[
v_r=(\chi_{-4}(r),\chi_{-3}(r),\chi_{12}(r)).
\]
Using the discriminant-12 character table,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
v_1&=(1,1,1),\\
v_5&=(1,-1,-1),\\
v_7&=(-1,1,-1),\\
v_{11}&=(-1,-1,1).
\end{aligned}}
\]

These are the four vertices of a regular tetrahedron centered at the origin.
Indeed,
\[
v_1+v_5+v_7+v_{11}=0,
\]
\[
\|v_r\|^2=3,
\]
and for distinct \(r,s\),
\[
\boxed{v_r\cdot v_s=-1.}
\]
Hence every edge has squared length
\[
\|v_r-v_s\|^2=3+3-2(-1)=8.
\]

The normalized tetrahedral vertices are \(v_r/\sqrt3\), but the integral coordinates are preferable for the root calculation.

---

## 2. Six pairwise differences

Choose one orientation for each of the six tetrahedral edges:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_{1,5}&=v_1-v_5=(0,2,2),\\
\beta_{1,7}&=v_1-v_7=(2,0,2),\\
\beta_{1,11}&=v_1-v_{11}=(2,2,0),\\
\beta_{5,7}&=v_5-v_7=(2,-2,0),\\
\beta_{5,11}&=v_5-v_{11}=(2,0,-2),\\
\beta_{7,11}&=v_7-v_{11}=(0,2,-2).
\end{aligned}
\]

Dividing by 2 gives the primitive integral root vectors
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
r_1&=(0,1,1),\\
r_2&=(1,0,1),\\
r_3&=(1,1,0),\\
r_4&=(1,-1,0),\\
r_5&=(1,0,-1),\\
r_6&=(0,1,-1).
\end{aligned}}
\]
Together with their negatives,
\[
\boxed{\Phi=\{\pm r_1,\ldots,\pm r_6\}.}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{\Phi=\{(\pm1,\pm1,0),(\pm1,0,\pm1),(0,\pm1,\pm1)\}.}
\]
There are exactly twelve roots.

Every root has squared length
\[
\boxed{\|\alpha\|^2=2.}
\]

This is the standard \(D_3\) realization, and \(D_3\cong A_3\).

---

## 3. Explicit A3 simple roots

Take the vertex chain
\[
1\to5\to7\to11.
\]
Define
\[
\alpha_1=\frac{v_1-v_5}{2}=(0,1,1),
\]
\[
\alpha_2=\frac{v_5-v_7}{2}=(1,-1,0),
\]
\[
\alpha_3=\frac{v_7-v_{11}}2=(0,1,-1).
\]
Then
\[
\alpha_i\cdot\alpha_i=2,
\]
\[
\alpha_1\cdot\alpha_2=-1,
\qquad
\alpha_2\cdot\alpha_3=-1,
\qquad
\alpha_1\cdot\alpha_3=0.
\]
Therefore their Cartan matrix is
\[
\boxed{
C=\begin{pmatrix}
2&-1&0\\
-1&2&-1\\
0&-1&2
\end{pmatrix},}
\]
which is exactly the Cartan matrix of \(A_3\).

The positive roots generated by these simple roots are
\[
\boxed{
\alpha_1,\ \alpha_2,\ \alpha_3,\
\alpha_1+\alpha_2,\
\alpha_2+\alpha_3,\
\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3.}
\]
Explicitly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_1&=(0,1,1),\\
\alpha_2&=(1,-1,0),\\
\alpha_3&=(0,1,-1),\\
\alpha_1+\alpha_2&=(1,0,1),\\
\alpha_2+\alpha_3&=(1,0,-1),\\
\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3&=(1,1,0),
\end{aligned}
\]
which are exactly the six primitive tetrahedral edge differences above.

Thus
\[
\boxed{\Phi=\Phi(A_3).}
\]

## 4. Reflections in the simple roots

Since \(\alpha_i^2=2\), the root reflection is
\[
s_{\alpha}(x)=x-(x\cdot\alpha)\alpha.
\]
Its matrix is
\[
S_\alpha=I-\alpha\alpha^T.
\]

For \(\alpha_1=(0,1,1)\),
\[
\boxed{S_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&0&-1\\
0&-1&0
\end{pmatrix}.}
\]

For \(\alpha_2=(1,-1,0)\),
\[
\boxed{S_2=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.}
\]

For \(\alpha_3=(0,1,-1)\),
\[
\boxed{S_3=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&1&0
\end{pmatrix}=P_F.}
\]

They satisfy the Coxeter relations
\[
\boxed{S_1^2=S_2^2=S_3^2=I,}
\]
\[
\boxed{(S_1S_2)^3=(S_2S_3)^3=I,}
\]
\[
\boxed{(S_1S_3)^2=I.}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\langle S_1,S_2,S_3\rangle=W(A_3)\cong S_4.}
\]

---

## 5. The simple reflections are already in the v13.483 matrix group

The matrices above are signed permutation matrices generated by \(D_5,D_7,D_{11},P_A,P_F\).

We have directly
\[
S_3=P_F.
\]
Also
\[
S_1=D_5P_F,
\]
because
\[
D_5P_F=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&0&-1\\
0&-1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The middle reflection \(S_2\) is the reflection swapping the first two character axes; in the S3 subgroup it is the corresponding conjugate of \(P_F\):
\[
S_2=P_A^2P_FP_A
\]
(up to the equivalent convention for multiplication order; its explicit matrix above fixes the identification unambiguously).

Thus every simple root reflection belongs to the same group
\[
\boxed{\langle D_5,D_7,P_A,P_F\rangle.}
\]
Conversely this group has order 24 and preserves \(\Phi\), so it is precisely the Weyl group.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{V_4\rtimes S_3\cong S_4\cong W(A_3).}
\]

## 6. Action on the tetrahedral vertices

Each v13.483 matrix permutes the four vectors
\[
\mathcal V=\{v_1,v_5,v_7,v_{11}\}.
\]

For the V4 matrices:
\[
D_5v_1=v_5,
\]
and multiplication of character values gives generally
\[
\boxed{D_rv_s=v_{rs}.}
\]
Thus the V4 subgroup acts regularly on the four tetrahedral vertices.

The S3 matrices fix \(v_1=(1,1,1)\) and permute the other three vertices. For example \(P_A\) cyclically permutes the coordinates and hence cycles \(v_5,v_7,v_{11}\), while \(P_F\) swaps the appropriate two.

Therefore the full signed-permutation group acts faithfully as all permutations of the four vertices:
\[
\boxed{\mathcal V\curvearrowleft S_4.}
\]

---

## 7. Action on roots

Because every root is half a vertex difference,
\[
\alpha_{r,s}=\frac{v_r-v_s}{2},
\]
for any group matrix \(M_g\),
\[
M_g\alpha_{r,s}
=\frac{M_gv_r-M_gv_s}{2}
=\frac{v_{g(r)}-v_{g(s)}}2
=\alpha_{g(r),g(s)}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{M_g\Phi=\Phi\qquad(g\in S_4).}
\]

The same matrices from the arithmetic Hadamard representation therefore act as root-system automorphisms, and the root reflections generate the full group.

## 8. Root-system axioms

For completeness:

1. \(\Phi\) is finite, spans \(\mathbb R^3\), and does not contain 0.
2. If \(\alpha\in\Phi\), the only scalar multiples of \(\alpha\) in \(\Phi\) are \(\pm\alpha\).
3. Every root has \(\alpha^2=2\), and reflection
   \[
   s_\alpha(x)=x-(x\cdot\alpha)\alpha
   \]
   preserves \(\Phi\).
4. For \(\alpha,\beta\in\Phi\),
   \[
   \frac{2\alpha\cdot\beta}{\alpha\cdot\alpha}=\alpha\cdot\beta\in\{0,\pm1,\pm2\}\subset\mathbb Z.
   \]

Thus \(\Phi\) is a reduced crystallographic root system. Its simple-root Cartan matrix identifies it uniquely as type \(A_3\).

## 9. Arithmetic interpretation

The tetrahedral vertices are not imported coordinates: they are exactly the three nontrivial discriminant-12 character values evaluated at the four V4 labels:
\[
\boxed{v_r=(\chi_{-4}(r),\chi_{-3}(r),\chi_{12}(r)).}
\]

The A3 roots are therefore normalized differences between character-sign states:
\[
\boxed{\alpha_{r,s}=\frac12\bigl(v_r-v_s\bigr).}
\]

This yields the exact chain
\[
\boxed{
U(12)\text{ character table}
\longrightarrow
\text{regular tetrahedron}
\longrightarrow
A_3\cong D_3\text{ roots}
\longrightarrow
W(A_3)\cong S_4.
}
\]

The same 3x3 signed-permutation matrices derived from the affine Hadamard action are exactly the Weyl-group matrices preserving this root system.

Guardrail: this is an exact representation-theoretic construction in character space. It does not by itself identify the A3 roots with physical cone-shell directions, nor does it alter the distinct arithmetic meanings of \(\chi_{12}\), \(\chi_{-3}\), and \(\chi_{-4}\).
