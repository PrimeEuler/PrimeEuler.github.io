# Cone Derivation Ledger v13.545 — Null rays as cyclic orderings: explicit S4/C4 model

## Status
Exact finite-group/coset calculation, building on corrected v13.543 and externally audited v13.544. This replaces the failed tetrahedral-edge interpretation by the correct degree-six S4/C4 action.

## 1. Verified six-ray action
Use the six projective null rays from v13.540/v13.543:

\[
a=[1:0:1],\quad b=[1:0:-1],\quad c=[0:1:1],\quad d=[0:1:-1],\quad e=[1:i:0],\quad f=[1:-i:0].
\]

For the determinant-one subgroup H ≅ S4, retain the verified generators

\[
r=(a\ c\ b\ d),\qquad s=(a\ f\ d)(b\ e\ c).
\]

Thus r has order 4 and s has order 3.

## 2. Cyclic orderings of the four residue labels
Let

\[
\Omega=\{1,5,7,11\}.
\]

An oriented cyclic ordering is a 4-tuple containing every element of Ω exactly once, modulo cyclic rotation only. Reversal is NOT identified. There are

\[
\frac{4!}{4}=6
\]

such cyclic orderings.

Take the vertex permutations

\[
R=(1\ 5\ 7\ 11),\qquad S=(5\ 11\ 7),
\]

with S fixing 1. They generate S4.

S4 acts on cyclic orderings by relabeling every entry, followed by cyclic rotation back to a chosen representative beginning with 1.

## 3. Explicit ray ↔ cyclic-ordering bijection
The following bijection Φ is equivariant for r↦R and s↦S:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{null ray}&\text{oriented cyclic ordering modulo rotation}\\
\hline
a&[1,5,11,7]\\
b&[1,7,11,5]\\
c&[1,11,5,7]\\
d&[1,7,5,11]\\
e&[1,5,7,11]\\
f&[1,11,7,5]
\end{array}}
\]

Here brackets mean cyclic class, so e.g.

\[
[1,5,7,11]=[5,7,11,1]=[7,11,1,5]=[11,1,5,7].
\]

The reverse ordering is distinct:

\[
[1,5,7,11]\ne[1,11,7,5].
\]

In particular e and f are the two opposite orientations of the same underlying 4-cycle.

## 4. Check of the order-four generator
Applying R=(1 5 7 11) by relabeling gives

\[
R:a\mapsto c\mapsto b\mapsto d\mapsto a,
\]

while

\[
R:e=e,\qquad R:f=f.
\]

Hence on the six cyclic classes

\[
R=(a\ c\ b\ d)(e)(f),
\]

exactly matching the cone generator r.

The reason e and f are fixed is transparent: e is the cyclic order generated by R itself,

\[
e=[1,5,7,11],
\]

and f is its reverse,

\[
f=[1,11,7,5].
\]

Relabeling either by R only rotates the cycle.

## 5. Check of the order-three generator
For

\[
S=(5\ 11\ 7),\qquad S(1)=1,
\]

direct relabeling gives

\[
S=(a\ f\ d)(b\ e\ c),
\]

which exactly matches the verified cone action of s.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\Phi(r\cdot x)=R\cdot\Phi(x),\qquad \Phi(s\cdot x)=S\cdot\Phi(x)}
\]

for every ray x. Since r,s generate H and R,S generate S4, Φ is fully S4-equivariant.

## 6. Coset interpretation
Choose the base cyclic ordering

\[
e=[1,5,7,11].
\]

Its stabilizer is exactly

\[
\operatorname{Stab}(e)=\langle R\rangle
=\langle(1\ 5\ 7\ 11)\rangle\cong C_4.
\]

Hence the six cyclic orderings are canonically the homogeneous S4-set

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_6\cong S_4/\langle R\rangle\cong S_4/C_4.}
\]

Transporting through Φ gives

\[
\boxed{\mathcal N_6\cong_{S_4}\mathcal C_6\cong S_4/C_4.}
\]

This is the explicit model requested by v13.543.

## 7. Relation to the failed edge model
The standard six tetrahedral edges form S4/V4, not S4/C4. The distinction is visible on an order-four element:

- on cyclic orderings S4/C4: cycle type 4+1+1;
- on tetrahedral edges S4/V4: cycle type 4+2.

Thus the cyclic-ordering model explains the exact cycle-type obstruction found in v13.543.

## 8. Structural interpretation
The six cone null rays do not encode unordered pairs of residue labels. They encode oriented cyclic arrangements of all four residue labels, modulo quarter-turn/rotation.

Equivalently:

\[
\boxed{
\text{null ray}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{oriented cyclic ordering of }\{1,5,7,11\}
\text{ modulo }C_4.
}
\]

The stabilizing C4 is generated by the 4-cycle R=(1 5 7 11). This makes the recurring order-four structure intrinsic to the six-ray geometry rather than an artifact of cardinality matching.

## 9. Guardrails
1. This is an exact S4-set identification after choosing the generator correspondence r↦R, s↦S and one basepoint. It is not claimed to be canonical without those choices.
2. Reversal of cyclic orientation is not quotiented out; doing so would collapse six classes to three.
3. This does not identify the six rays with A3 root/edge directions; v13.543 proves that S4-set identification impossible.
4. The result does not by itself prove that the C4 here is cyclotomic multiplication by i or the projective arithmetic-negation C4. Those operator-level identifications require a separate intertwiner.
