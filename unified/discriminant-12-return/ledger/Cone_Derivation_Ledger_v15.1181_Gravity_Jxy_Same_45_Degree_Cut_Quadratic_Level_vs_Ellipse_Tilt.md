## Gate 1095 — the cut has precisely the same orientation for both parabolas
Let Q(T,X,Y)=T²−X²−Y² and fix the affine plane **T−X=K** with K>0, i.e. T=X+K. This plane's normal is null for the Minkowski quadratic form; equivalently its Euclidean T/X-plane orientation is 45 degrees. On the **null cone** Q=0, its intersection is **Y²=K²+2KX**. When K=k integer this is an original ±1/2-vertex EM acceleration parabola. When K=k+1/2 it is the quarter-interleaved ±1/4-vertex parabola. Changing K translates the cutting plane without changing its orientation.

For the **same** cutting plane at the **same K**, but on the quadratic level Q=mu (constant), intersection is instead
**Y²=K²+2KX−mu**.
This remains an exactly parabolic section for all mu, including **mu=1/4**. Its vertex is **Xv(mu,K)=−K/2+mu/(2K)**, its focal parameter p=K/2, and its focus is **Xf(mu,K)=mu/(2K)**. Original EM and quarter acceleration rows at mu=0 have focus Xf=0; Jxy at mu=1/4 has Xf=1/(8K)=1/[4(2k+1)], recovering v15.1179. Thus owner is right that the three parabolas share the same cutting angle (the K values vary between integer and half-integer rows); the shift comes from the **level of the quadratic**, not an additional tilt of the cutting plane.

## Gate 1097 — comparison with ellipse-tilt degree of freedom
Sandbox v15.1172 cuts the null cone with **T cos gamma+Y sin gamma=c**. Solve T=(c−sin gamma Y)/cos gamma and insert into Q=mu:
**Q−mu=(c−sin gamma Y)²/cos²gamma−X²−Y²−mu=0**.
The Y² quadratic coefficient is **tan²gamma−1** and the X² coefficient remains −1. Therefore a cut at **gamma=45°** removes the Y² term and is parabolic, for **both mu=0 and mu=1/4**; mu shifts only the constant term. The T−X=K plane is equivalent by rotation of the spatial X,Y directions to a null-direction plane at gamma=45°, although the two parameterizations are not the same labelled plane without that spatial rotation. Altering gamma away from 45° changes the quadratic shape/eccentricity, whereas altering mu with gamma fixed at 45° simply translates the parabola and its focus. One operation changes the plane normal, the other changes the quadratic level. The new focus shift is consequently **akin to** shifting the conic section in the same common geometry, but **not itself an independently dynamical tilt/eccentricity parameter**.

A simple experimental discriminator would have to measure a physical change of conic *shape* or orientation rather than infer a new force from a representation-space 1/4 translation.
