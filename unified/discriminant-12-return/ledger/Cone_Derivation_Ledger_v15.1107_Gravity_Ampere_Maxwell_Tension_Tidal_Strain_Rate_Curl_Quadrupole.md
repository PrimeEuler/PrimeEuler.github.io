## Gate 969 — historical Ampère tension versus standard Maxwell stress
The user's prompt 'rate of change ... Ampere tension' can mean two distinct things. Historically **Ampère tension** refers to purported longitudinal force within a current-bearing conductor under Ampère's element-force picture. Such microscopic longitudinal force-distribution descriptions have a different status from the accepted Lorentz force on charges and Maxwell electromagnetic stress; do NOT infer a novel local longitudinal force from the historical phrase. Relevant historical/experimental sources: Physics Letters A (1983), 'First indication of ampere tension in solid electric conductors', https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0375960183907600 ; Physics Letters A (1985), 'Ampere and Lorentz forces', https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0375960185905900. In the established Maxwell field-stress picture, magnetic field lines transmit tension of magnitude B²/(2mu0) along their direction and pressure transversely. This is an **interpretation of the conventional stress tensor**, not an added force law.

## Gate 970 — the apparent cancellation around a perfectly circular magnetic ring
At one transverse radius r in the (y,z)-plane around the x axis, take B_r=0, B_phi=B0(r), so B_y=-B0 sin(phi), B_z=B0 cos(phi), B_x=0. For magnetic-only stress, \mathcal T_yy-\mathcal T_zz=B_z²-B_y²=B0² cos(2phi) and \mathcal T_yz=-B_y B_z=(B0²/2)sin(2phi). Integrating each over an entire ideal circular ring phi in [0,2pi) gives **zero**. Thus a perfectly axisymmetric curl circle, while it stores nonzero magnetic energy/tension, has no integrated first-order TT stress coupling to a co-axial gravitational plus/cross tidal wave. This is a precise spin-2 angular selection rule, not proof that any local magnetic field cannot respond or any non-collinear gravitational wave is blind.
