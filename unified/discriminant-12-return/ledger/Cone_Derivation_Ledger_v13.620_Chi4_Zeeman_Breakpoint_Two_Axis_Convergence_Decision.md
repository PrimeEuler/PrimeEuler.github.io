## 4. Numerical decision

The breakpoint characteristic is projectively stable under the tested degree/order refinement even while its first-kind matrices become increasingly ill-conditioned. The changes from degree 10 through 14 are tiny compared with the O(1)--O(10) separation from the Zeeman characteristic.

Meanwhile increasing j does not drive the full normalized Zeeman characteristic toward the stabilized breakpoint result at any tested A.

Therefore the v13.616 decision rule selects the carrier-failure branch:

This is stronger than v13.611's A-drift diagnosis and v13.615's single-resolution baseline: the discrepancy survives independent refinement of both axes.

## 5. Important qualification

This is a numerical rejection of the **bare nodal compression**, not of SU(2) as a carrier.

The failure mechanism is structurally plausible: the Jz trapezoidal compression preserves node ordering and SU(2) ladder geometry, but it does not enforce the Section-8 Friedrichs endpoint/domain conditions that the breakpoint Fredholm construction imposes explicitly.

Therefore the next construction should not abandon SU(2). It should replace bare nodal sampling by a Galerkin/intertwining map from a domain-faithful basis into the finite spin-j carrier.

## 6. Next gate

Construct a Dirichlet/Friedrichs-adapted Galerkin basis on [-A,A], for example parity-separated sine/cosine modes satisfying the endpoint trace, and map its finite coefficient space unitarily into the spin-j carrier.

Then compare:
1. the Galerkin finite characteristic against the breakpoint Fredholm characteristic at fixed A;
2. the same Galerkin operator after unitary transport into the SU(2) carrier;
3. only after those agree, resume the A-robust beta-zero branch test.

The unitary transport cannot change the Galerkin characteristic spectrum; its purpose is to retain the SU(2) carrier without sacrificing the Section-8 domain geometry.
