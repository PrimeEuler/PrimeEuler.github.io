# Part I — q=5 Gaussian operator plane

## 3. Exact restriction of all q=5 local operators

For

\[
(T_{r,\sigma}f)(k)=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}f(rk+j)-\sigma f(k),

every q=5 local operator preserves `span_Z{b_1,b_2}`. The restricted matrices are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}

Thus the q=5 determinant alphabet is

\[
\boxed{0,2,4.}

In particular there is no determinant-one nonzero local support operator.

## 4. Gaussian generator

Define

\[
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{J^2=-I,\qquad J^4=I.}
\]

The four determinant-two letters are

\[
R_{2,-}=I+J,
\qquad
R_{2,+}=-I+J,
\]

\[
R_{3,-}=I-J,
\qquad
R_{3,+}=-I-J.
\]

The determinant-four letters are `\pm2I`, and the remaining two letters are zero.

Therefore the entire restricted q=5 operator ring lies in

\[
\boxed{\mathbf Z[J]\cong\mathbf Z[i],}
\]

the Gaussian integer ring.

Under

\[
J\leftrightarrow i,
\]

the determinant is exactly the Gaussian norm:

\[
\boxed{
det(aI+bJ)=a^2+b^2=N_{\mathbf Q(i)/\mathbf Q}(a+bi).
}
\]

The local support alphabet corresponds to

\[
0,\quad \pm2,\quad \pm1\pm i.
\]

Hence the nonzero local norms are precisely 2 and 4.

## 5. Gaussian normal form

Let

\[
P:=I+J\leftrightarrow1+i.
\]

Since

\[
(1+i)^2=2i,
\]
we have the matrix identity

\[
\boxed{P^2=2J.}
\]

Every determinant-two local letter is a Gaussian unit times P, because

\[
1-i=-i(1+i),
\]

\[
-1+i=i(1+i),
\]

\[
-1-i=-(1+i).
\]

Also

\[
2=-i(1+i)^2.
\]

Therefore every noncollapsed q=5 support word has the exact form

\[
\boxed{R_w=P^aJ^j,\qquad a\ge1\text{ for every nonempty word},\quad j\in\mathbf Z/4\mathbf Z,}
\]

with the empty word corresponding to a=0.

Equivalently, after extracting powers of 2,

\[
R_w=2^mP^\epsilon J^j,
\qquad \epsilon\in\{0,1\}.
\]

The determinant is

\[
\boxed{\Delta_2(F_w)=|\det R_w|=2^a.}
\]

Thus modulo Gaussian unit phase, the entire noncollapsed q=5 support orbit is the one-dimensional depth ray

\[
\boxed{\mathbf N_0.}
\]

The prototype is the unique depth-zero state.

## 6. Exact no-recurrence explanation at q=5

The Gaussian unit group is

\[
\mathbf Z[i]^\times=\{\pm1,\pm i\}\cong C_4.
\]

But none of those units occurs as a nontrivial local support operator. Every nonzero support letter is divisible by `1+i` and has norm at least 2.

Therefore every nonempty noncollapsed support word satisfies

\[
\Delta_2\ge2,
\]

while the prototype has

\[
\Delta_2=1.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\text{q=5 has no nontrivial support recurrence because its local support alphabet misses the Gaussian unit group.}}
\]

This upgrades the earlier determinantal no-return statement into a ring-theoretic explanation.

## 8. Recurrence criterion on a primitive rank-two operator plane

The comparison isolates a general mechanism.

Suppose a prototype fiber spans a primitive rank-two lattice and each local support operator restricts to an integral 2x2 matrix. If decorated-fiber equivalence quotients by a finite phase subgroup of GL_2(Z), then a nontrivial recurrent local support step can occur only if its restricted matrix is unimodular:

\[
\boxed{|\det R|=1.}
\]

Indeed prototype equivalence preserves the second determinantal divisor, while

\[
\Delta_2(RF)=|\det R|\Delta_2(F)
\]

on a primitive invariant rank-two plane.

Thus local units are the only possible recurrent letters.

For q=5:

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A_5^{\rm unit}=\varnothing.}
\]

For q=7:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_7^{\rm unit}
=\{(2,-1),(3,-1),(4,-1),(5,-1)\},
}
\]

which is exactly the preserving alphabet found in v13.240.

So the earlier empirical-looking contrast is now an exact algebraic dichotomy:

\[
\boxed{
\text{support recurrence exists precisely where the local support alphabet intersects the unit group nontrivially.}
}
\]

This statement is exact for the q=5 and q=7 primitive rank-two prototype planes studied here.

## 9. Ramification/inertia interpretation of depth

The depth directions also acquire a clean algebraic meaning.

At q=5, in Z[i],

\[
2=-i(1+i)^2,
\]

so 2 is ramified. Every noncollapsed local support step carries at least one factor of the ramified Gaussian prime `(1+i)`. Consequently there is only one independent valuation direction, giving the depth ray N_0.

At q=7, in the Eisenstein ring Z[zeta_6], the norm-3 generator lies above the ramified rational prime 3, while the scalar 2 has norm 4 and gives a second independent prime direction. Hence the depth quotient is N_0^2.

So the dimensions of the depth quotients reflect the number of independent prime-ideal valuation directions actually present in the local support alphabet:

\[
\boxed{q=5:\ 1\text{ depth direction},\qquad q=7:\ 2\text{ depth directions}.}
\]
