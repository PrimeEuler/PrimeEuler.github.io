## v14.950 — Opposite squeeze: self-adjoint spectrum is continuous. VERIFIED exactly, by independent from-scratch re-derivation of the key operator identity.

This closes `v14.949`'s open question (does the quantum opposite-dilation generator `\hat p_s` admit a well-defined self-adjoint realization, and if so what is its spectrum?). The entry claims that in `(\rho,s)` coordinates the Jacobian is `2\rho` and the symmetrized generator `P_s=(up_u+p_uu)/2-(vp_v+p_vv)/2=-i\hbar(u\partial_u-v\partial_v)` becomes exactly `-i\hbar\partial_s` — i.e. the `\rho`-dependence drops out completely — making it unitarily equivalent to the ordinary momentum operator on `L^2(\mathbb R,ds)`, hence essentially self-adjoint with purely absolutely continuous spectrum `\mathbb R` and no point spectrum.

I independently re-derived every step rather than taking it on faith. **Jacobian:** with `u=\rho e^s,v=\rho e^{-s}`, the Jacobian determinant `\partial(u,v)/\partial(\rho,s)=\det\begin{pmatrix}e^s&\rho e^s\\e^{-s}&-\rho e^{-s}\end{pmatrix}=-\rho-\rho=-2\rho`, so `|{\cdot}|=2\rho` exactly, matching the claim. **Operator identity:** first, `(up_u+p_uu)/2=-i\hbar(u\partial_u+\tfrac12)` (from `\partial_u(uf)=f+u\partial_uf`), so the `+\tfrac12` terms cancel between the `u` and `v` pieces, giving `P_s=-i\hbar(u\partial_u-v\partial_v)` exactly as claimed. Then, computing `\partial_u,\partial_v$ in terms of `\partial_\rho,\partial_s$ by the chain rule from `\rho=\sqrt{uv},s=\frac12\ln(u/v)$ (`\partial\rho/\partial u=e^{-s}/2,\ \partial s/\partial u=e^{-s}/(2\rho)$, and the symmetric expressions for `v`), I found `u\partial_u=\frac{\rho}{2}\partial_\rho+\frac12\partial_s` and `v\partial_v=\frac{\rho}{2}\partial_\rho-\frac12\partial_s`, so `u\partial_u-v\partial_v=\partial_s` **exactly** — the `\rho`-derivative terms cancel completely, confirming `P_s=-i\hbar\partial_s` with zero residual.

Given this exact reduction, the rest is standard, elementary spectral theory, correctly applied: `-i\hbar\,d/ds` on `L^2(\mathbb R,ds)` (the full line, no boundary identification) is the textbook momentum operator — essentially self-adjoint on a suitable dense domain, purely absolutely continuous spectrum equal to all of `\mathbb R`, no normalizable eigenfunctions. The entry's discussion of what an artificial compactification `s\sim s+L` would do (discrete spectrum `2\pi\hbar(n+\theta/2\pi)/L` with a boundary holonomy `\theta`, as in the standard particle-on-a-ring problem) is correct and explicitly and appropriately flagged as an *extra* assumption not present in the actual `s\in\mathbb R` configuration space. The conclusion — that the exact SU(2)/SU(1,1) Casimir invariants found earlier in this chain do not, by themselves, quantize this charge — follows correctly and closes the question raised in `v14.949` with a clean negative result rather than leaving it dangling.
