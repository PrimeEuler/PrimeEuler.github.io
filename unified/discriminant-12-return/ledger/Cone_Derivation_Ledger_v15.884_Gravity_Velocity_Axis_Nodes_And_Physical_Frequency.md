**Critical new input:** the horizontal X axis of the Cone picture is expressly measured in **metres per second**, and nodes sit on that axis. Earlier discussions tacitly treated node coordinates as dimensionless turns, phase, or time; those interpretations are not equivalent. This gate corrects the physical interpretation without rewriting previous records. No claim is made that Sandbox v15.860 itself already proved that this X axis is v; its ledger states that rays carry the ±c velocity tags.

**Gate 533 — axis node velocities can have real SI units.** If the X coordinate is v, and a specified, independently justified axis calibration gives u=v/c, then u=1/2 and u=1/4 correspond respectively to v=c/2 and v=c/4. The normalization v/c and the identification of which parabolic nodes occur at these fractions require a physical rule, not merely the diagram's appearance. These are velocity locations, not frequencies in hertz. The speed of light c has dimensions m/s, so no positive-frequency s^-1 is inferable from c alone.

**Gate 534 — dynamical traversal is needed to measure temporal node spacing.** For a periodic *velocity-axis* phase profile theta(v)=2pi v/V with velocity interval V per cycle, a trajectory v(t) reads out instantaneous phase angular speed theta_dot=(2pi/V) (dv/dt). The formal instantaneous crossing frequency is **f_inst=(dv/dt)/V**, sign-sensitive and not necessarily periodic or constant. In particular for a constant acceleration a along the axis, adjacent velocity nodes spaced V/2 are crossed in time Delta t=V/(2a), and the formal frequency is a/V. However a monotone accelerated trajectory crossing a stationary profile is not by itself a self-sustained periodic oscillator, and unrestricted constant a violates |v|<c eventually. The arbitrary acceleration can be rescaled while every node's velocity coordinate and the action circle's normalized geometry remain fixed.

For a more physical constant *proper* acceleration a0 (1D relativistic motion from rest), a(v)=a0(1-v²/c²)^(3/2) in lab coordinate time, and **t(v)=v/[a0 sqrt(1-v²/c²)]**. Node-to-node time separations vary with v and depend on a0; they cannot be read from the speed-axis spacing alone.

**Gate 535 — null-ray tag is an axis calibration, not motion along that axis.** A massless ray propagates in inertial vacuum at speed c, rather than accelerating through subluminal velocity nodes on the X-axis. Its spacetime trajectory x(t)=ct determines distance traveled once a duration is known, but the constant velocity v=c yields dv/dt=0, so traversing a phase profile parameterized *by v* does not happen for a free null ray. Distinguish x as a spatial position coordinate (metres) from X as the user's velocity coordinate (m/s). Likewise the v15.883 quantum phase relation theta=Et/hbar remains correct when E is supplied, but node positions on a speed X axis are not automatically the equal-time zeros of sin(Et/hbar).
