## 1. Prime-power local contact factor

For a prime residue \(g=p\bmod12\in U(12)\cong V_4\), define
\[
G_{g,e}=\sum_{a=0}^{e}e_{g^a}.
\]
If \(g=1\),
\[
G_{1,e}=(e+1)e_1.
\]
If \(g\neq1\), then \(g^2=1\), so
\[
\boxed{
G_{g,e}
=
\left(\left\lfloor\frac e2\right\rfloor+1\right)e_1
+
\left\lceil\frac e2\right\rceil e_g.
}
\]
Hence odd exponent is exactly uniform on \(\{1,g\}\), while even exponent has one extra identity-class divisor.

## 2. Exact character annihilation criterion

For any of the four real characters modulo 12,
\[
D_\chi(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\chi(d)
=\prod_{p^e\parallel n}\sum_{a=0}^e\chi(p)^a.
\]
Since \(\chi(p)=\pm1\),
\[
\boxed{
D_\chi(n)=0
\iff
\exists\,p^e\parallel n:
\chi(p)=-1
\text{ and }
e\text{ odd}.
}
\]

## 3. Normalized attenuation

Let
\[
\tau(n)=\prod_{p^e\parallel n}(e+1),
\qquad
\rho_\chi(n)=\frac{D_\chi(n)}{\tau(n)}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\rho_\chi(n)=
\begin{cases}
0,
&\exists\,p^e\parallel n:\chi(p)=-1,\ e\text{ odd},\\[2mm]
\displaystyle\prod_{\substack{p^e\parallel n\\\chi(p)=-1}}\frac1{e+1},
&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
\]
Thus:
- odd negative exponent annihilates the character channel;
- even negative exponent preserves the channel but attenuates it by \(1/(e+1)\) after normalization.

## 4. General contact-vector reconstruction

Let
\[
P_r=\frac{C_r}{\tau(n)},
\qquad r=1,5,7,11.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}
P_1\\P_5\\P_7\\P_{11}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\frac14H_4
\begin{pmatrix}
1\\\rho_{-4}\\\rho_{-3}\\\rho_{12}
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]
Explicitly,
\[
P_1=\frac14(1+\rho_{-4}+\rho_{-3}+\rho_{12}),
\]
\[
P_5=\frac14(1+\rho_{-4}-\rho_{-3}-\rho_{12}),
\]
\[
P_7=\frac14(1-\rho_{-4}+\rho_{-3}-\rho_{12}),
\]
\[
P_{11}=\frac14(1-\rho_{-4}-\rho_{-3}+\rho_{12}).
\]
This classifies the complete unit-residue divisor-contact distribution for arbitrary prime exponents.

## 5. Support subgroup versus weight uniformity

Let \(H(n)\le V_4\) be generated by the nontrivial prime residues dividing \(n\). Every local prime-power factor with \(e\ge1\) has positive coefficients on both \(1\) and its residue direction, so
\[
\boxed{
C_r(n)>0\iff r\in H(n).
}
\]
Therefore the support subgroup depends only on which residue directions occur, not their exponent parity.

Uniformity is different. It requires vanishing of all character coefficients nontrivial on \(H(n)\), so it is controlled by odd-exponent annihilation. The squarefree theorem v13.382 is recovered because every present exponent is odd.

## 6. Examples

For \(p\equiv5\pmod{12}\):

- \(n=p^{2m}\):
\[
\boxed{(C_1,C_5,C_7,C_{11})=(m+1,m,0,0).}
\]
- \(n=p^{2m+1}\):
\[
\boxed{(C_1,C_5,C_7,C_{11})=(m+1,m+1,0,0).}
\]

With two independent nontrivial residue directions present, support is all of \(V_4\). Full uniformity
\[
(C_1,C_5,C_7,C_{11})=\frac{\tau(n)}4(1,1,1,1)
\]
occurs exactly when every nontrivial character is annihilated by at least one odd-exponent prime in a negative character class.

## 7. Structural separation

The general-exponent contact distribution has three independent layers:
\[
\boxed{\text{support subgroup}}
\]
from presence of residue directions,
\[
\boxed{\text{channel annihilation}}
\]
from odd exponents, and
\[
\boxed{\text{channel attenuation}}
\]
from even negative exponents.

This is the exact extension of the squarefree orbit theorem.
