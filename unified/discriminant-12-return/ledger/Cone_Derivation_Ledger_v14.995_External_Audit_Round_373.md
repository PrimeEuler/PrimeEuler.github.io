## v14.994 — Exact quadratic-action identity between the AM and elastic (`η=3/2`) models. VERIFIED correct, after catching and fixing a genuine error of my own.

This is the substantial technical content of the round. `v14.994` claims that `W_{AM}(B)=C\,\mathrm{tr}\,B/3` and `W_{el}(B)=C(\det B)^{1/3}(1+\eta J(B))` at `\eta=3/2` agree in value and in their first and second derivatives at every isotropic point `B=\beta I`, and therefore that the full quadratic ADM actions for the two matter models — sharing the same Einstein–Hilbert sector and the same `\phi^I=z^I` background — must be identical, making `v14.935`'s and `v14.993`'s differing reduced phonon spectra mutually incompatible.

**My own error, caught and corrected.** On my first attempt to check this independently, using the established convention `L_m=-C\,b^{2/3}(1+\eta J)` with `b=\sqrt{\det B}` (so `b^{2/3}=(\det B)^{1/3}`), I mistakenly coded the elastic matter term as `C\cdot[(\det B)^{1/3}]^{2/3}\cdot(1+\eta J)` — raising the already-correct `(\det B)^{1/3}` factor to a *second*, spurious power of `2/3`. This produced an apparent, striking contradiction: computed this way, `W_{AM}` and `W_{el}` disagreed even in their *values* at a generic isotropic background (`C\beta` vs. `C\beta^{2/3}`), and their gradients and Hessians differed by large, generic amounts — which would have meant `v14.994`'s central claim was simply false. I did not report this without checking it against the established convention first: re-reading `v14.935`'s own frozen `NOTE.md` (`b=\sqrt{\det B}`, so `b^{2/3}=(\det B)^{1/3}` directly, with no further power applied) confirmed my coding was the error, not the physics.

**Corrected check.** With `G_B:=(\det B)^{1/3}` used directly (no extra power) as the shared prefactor `W_{el}=C\,G_B\,(1+\tfrac32 J)`, `W_{AM}=C\,G_B\cdot(A_B/G_B)`, I verified in `sympy`, for a fully general two-independent-eigenvalue reduction (`B=\mathrm{diag}(k_1,k_1,k_3)`, covering the scalar-sector case) and again for `B=\mathrm{diag}(k_1,k_2,k_2)` (the vector-sector case), that at the isotropic point `k_1=k_2$ (or `k_1=k_3`) `=\beta`, for **arbitrary** `\beta`: the **values** match exactly (`C\beta` both), **every first partial derivative** matches exactly (`2C/3` and `C/3` in the scalar case; `C/3` and `2C/3` in the vector case), and **every second partial derivative (the full Hessian) matches exactly — all identically zero for both models** in both cases. This is a strictly stronger and more direct confirmation than a scale-invariant-ratio argument: it shows the two matter energy functions agree as functions of the raw metric-perturbation-dependent quantities `(k_1,k_3)` (or `(k_1,k_2)`) to full second order at *every* background scale `\beta`, not merely along one invariant direction. Since the Einstein–Hilbert sector, the background, and the functional dependence of `B` on the ADM fields are all shared between the two matter models, this means the complete quadratic (matter+gravity) ADM actions for the scalar and vector sectors **must** be identical between the AM-triplet and elastic-`\eta=3/2`models — confirming `v14.994`'s blocker claim is mathematically sound, not merely plausible.

**Consequence.** Given this, `v14.935`'s elastic-model scalar/vector reduction (`Q_{el}\tau^2=3k^2+4` uniformly, no kinetic-sign band) and `v14.993`'s AM-model reduction (`Q_S,Q_V` with a genuine negative-kinetic band below `k_{phys}=2H`) cannot both be exact results of the same underlying quadratic action. At least one of the two reduction procedures contains an error.

**A diagnosis, offered as evidence-weighted judgment, not a settled finding.** I have not, within this round, re-derived either full reduction end-to-end from scratch to pinpoint the fault with certainty — that is a substantial undertaking I flag as the necessary next step rather than claim to have completed. But I can offer a concrete, specific reason to suspect `v14.935`'s scalar-sector reduction is the more likely source of the discrepancy, drawn directly from this audit's own prior work: in Round 365 (`v14.938`), auditing `v14.935`'s `scalar_statespace.py`, I found that the scalar field `\psi`'s own Euler–Lagrange equation is **identically `0=0`** at the relevant background (`\kappa C=1,\dot a^2=1/3`) — not a genuine constraint at all, meaning `\psi` is not actually algebraically slaved by its own equation in that reduction (the script's `\alpha=-C01/C00` step there is a `0/0` ratio of floating-point noise, which happened not to corrupt the *final* reported numbers only because it was multiplied by an independently-exact zero elsewhere). By contrast, `v14.993`'s scalar reduction explicitly derives a **nontrivial** `\psi`-constraint (`\sqrt3\tau[(k^2-1/3)\psi+\pi/9-\tau\dot\pi/(9k^2)]=0`) and validates it against exact zero residuals on the original (unreduced) equations of motion — a materially different, and on its face more robust, methodology for exactly the sector where the two results diverge. This is circumstantial, not dispositive: it is possible both reductions are internally consistent but reach their stated gauge/variable identifications differently, or that the fault lies elsewhere entirely. I report it because it is the single most concrete lead this audit currently has, not as a verdict.
