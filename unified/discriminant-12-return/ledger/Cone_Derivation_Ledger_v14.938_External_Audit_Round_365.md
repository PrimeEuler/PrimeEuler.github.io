## v14.935 — K4 full SVT stability return (Sandbox). VERIFIED, with one methodological finding that does not affect the result.

This closes the `v14.928`/`v14.932`/`v14.933` handoff with a full ADM + solid quadratic action, lapse/shift eliminated by constraints, claiming every `η∈(3/8,3/2]` survives coupled tensor+vector+scalar stability. - **Vector sector.** I independently re-derived `\omega_V^2=m_T^2+c_T^2k_{phys}^2=8\eta/3+2\eta k^2` by hand from the payload's own printed kinetic/potential coefficients substituted at the background (`C\kappa=1,\dot a^2=1/3`), with no `sympy` needed — exact match to the claimed closed form and to the chain's own numeric cross-check (`ratio=1.000000`).
- **Scalar sector — the finding.** Re-deriving the scalar state-space reduction independently in `sympy` (keeping `\eta,k` symbolic rather than numeric), I found that the `\psi`-field's own Euler–Lagrange equation is **identically `0=0`** at the background `\kappa C=1,\dot a^2=1/3` — not a genuine constraint. The script's `alpha = -C01/C00` step therefore numerically divides two quantities that are each exactly zero (confirmed: both are rational functions of `k` that `sympy.simplify` reduces to `0` symbolically), and the `alpha` values the chain actually prints are floating-point noise from a `0/0` division (e.g. `alpha=-0.142857` at `\eta=0.75,k=1`, `alpha=0.573` at `\eta=0.375,k=1000` — neither is a meaningful quantity). **This does not corrupt the final answer**: `alpha` only enters `Ceff=C21+C20\cdot\mathrm{alpha}`, and `C20` is *also* exactly zero by the same symbolic identity, so the noise term `C20\cdot\mathrm{alpha}` is negligible (`\sim10^{-16}`) next to `C21` (`O(1)`–`O(10^4)` in the sampled range) and the reported `Ceff\approx C21` is correct regardless. I confirmed this by re-deriving `\omega_0^2=C21/A22` directly (bypassing `alpha` entirely) and it matches the claimed closed form `m_T^2+c_L^2k_{phys}^2` **exactly**, including the `\eta=3/8` marginal point (`\omega_0^2=1` for all `k`, confirmed exactly from my closed form), the exact friction identity `B21/A22=3` (giving `\lambda^2+3\lambda+\omega_0^2=0`, `\lambda=(-3\pm\sqrt5)/2` at `\eta=3/8`, matching exactly), and the `\eta=27/32` overdamped/underdamped threshold (from `\omega_0^2\to8\eta/3` as `k\to0`, setting `8\eta/3=9/4`). So: the entry's description of `\psi` as "slaved via `\psi=\alpha(k,\tau)P`" is not accurate — `\psi`'s own equation gives no such constraint, and the number used for `alpha` in the printed table is uncontrolled rounding noise — but the final dispersion relations, the verdict, and every quoted number are independently confirmed correct, because the ill-defined term happens to be multiplied by an independently-exact zero. This is worth fixing for robustness (check `C00,C01,C20` are symbolic zero before dividing, rather than dividing floats near machine epsilon) but is not a result-corrupting error.

**Verdict on v14.935: the physics conclusion stands, confirmed by independent re-derivation through a cleaner route; the described mechanism for `\psi` is inaccurate in a way that doesn't matter here.**
