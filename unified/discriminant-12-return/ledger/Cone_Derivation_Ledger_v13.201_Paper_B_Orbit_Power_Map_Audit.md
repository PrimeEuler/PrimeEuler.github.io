## 1. Executive result

Paper B contains a strong exact core, but its present headline claims overstate what has been proved on the positive-factor sheet and its power map is not canonical until a normalization of the line equation is fixed.

The strongest surviving exact core is:

\[
\boxed{
G_{a,b}=(a+b)L+(a-b)B_Y
}
\]

with characteristic polynomial

\[
\boxed{
\chi_G(\lambda)=\lambda(\lambda^2+4ab),
}
\]

and invariant plane

\[
\boxed{
(a-b)X+(a+b)T=c.
}
\]

For the **signed** cone section, the elliptic/hyperbolic cut is an orbit of the corresponding one-parameter Lorentz subgroup.  On Paper A's actual factor sheet, where

\[
Y=+\sqrt{xy}\ge0,
\]

the traced curve is generally only the \(Y\ge0\) half of that signed orbit.

The complex/split eigen-coordinate identities also survive, with the sign-safe ellipse statement

\[
\boxed{|\zeta_{a,b}|=|c|}
\]

rather than \(|\zeta|=c\) unless a positive normalization is imposed.

The current row/column conclusion “no power map exists at all” does **not** survive: the elementary coordinatewise map

\[
(x,y)\mapsto(x^n,y^n)
\]

already maps a row \(x=c\) to the row \(x=c^n\), and similarly for columns.  What fails in the parabolic case is specifically the **diagonal eigen-coordinate construction**, not the existence of every possible power map.

## 2. [D] Generator matrix and invariant axis

With

\[
L=\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B_Y=\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]

Paper B defines

\[
G_{a,b}=(a+b)L+(a-b)B_Y.
\]

Writing

\[
\alpha=a+b,\qquad \gamma=a-b,
\]

we have

\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}
0&-\alpha&0\\
\alpha&0&\gamma\\
0&\gamma&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its kernel contains

\[
m=(\gamma,0,-\alpha)
=(a-b,0,-(a+b)),
\]

because

\[
Gm=0.
\]

With \(\eta=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1)\),

\[
\langle m,(X,Y,T)\rangle_\eta
=(a-b)X+(a+b)T.
\]

Therefore this plane functional is exactly invariant under the flow of \(G_{a,b}\).

### [Audit] proof-text error

The current proof lists incorrect component equations for \(Gm=0\), including conditions of the form \(\gamma m_3=0\) and \(\gamma m_1=0\).  The actual equations are

\[
-\alpha m_2=0,
\qquad
\alpha m_1+\gamma m_3=0,
\qquad
\gamma m_2=0.
\]

The theorem's chosen kernel vector is correct; the written component derivation is not.

## 3. [D] Eigenvalue/conic-type bridge survives

Direct computation gives

\[
\boxed{
\chi_G(\lambda)=\lambda(\lambda^2+4ab).
}
\]

Hence:

- \(ab>0\): nonzero eigenvalues \(\pm2i\sqrt{ab}\), elliptic generator;
- \(ab<0\): nonzero eigenvalues \(\pm2\sqrt{-ab}\), hyperbolic generator;
- \(ab=0\): all eigenvalues zero, parabolic/nilpotent case.

The plane normal

\[
n=(a-b,0,a+b)
\]

has Lorentz norm

\[
\boxed{
\|n\|_\eta^2
=(a-b)^2-(a+b)^2
=-4ab.
}
\]

Thus Paper A's sign-of-\(ab\) conic classification and the Lorentz causal classification are genuinely the same invariant.

This is one of Paper B's strongest publication-worthy results.

## 4. [Audit] “the complete curve is a single orbit” needs a signed-sheet qualification

Paper A uses

\[
Y=\sqrt{xy}\ge0.
\]

The connected Lorentz flow generated by \(G_{a,b}\) acts naturally on the signed future cone

\[
T>0,
\qquad
X^2+Y^2=T^2,
\qquad
Y\in\mathbb R.
\]

It does not in general preserve the half-sheet \(Y\ge0\).

Examples:

1. For \(a=b\), \(G_{a,a}=2aL\) rotates the full \((X,Y)\)-circle.  Paper A's factor image contains only its upper semicircle \(Y\ge0\).
2. For \(a=-b\), \(G_{a,-a}=2aB_Y\) gives the full future hyperbola in \((Y,T)\), including positive and negative \(Y\).  The positive-factor image is the \(Y\ge0\) half.

Therefore the publication-safe statement is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The signed double of a nondegenerate Paper-A cut is a full }G_{a,b}\text{-orbit.}
}
\]

Paper A's actual multiplication-table trace is the portion of that orbit on \(Y\ge0\).

This distinction is structural, not cosmetic, because a continuous one-parameter group orbit cannot remain the full rotation while being restricted to only a semicircle.

## 6. [D] Eigen-coordinate identities survive, with a sign correction

For \(ab>0\),

\[
\zeta_{a,b}
=(a+b)X+(a-b)T+2i\sqrt{ab}\,Y.
\]

The row vector

\[
w^\top=(a+b,\ 2i\sqrt{ab},\ a-b)
\]

is a left eigenvector:

\[
w^\top G_{a,b}
=2i\sqrt{ab}\,w^\top.
\]

Therefore

\[
\zeta(t)=\zeta(0)e^{2i\sqrt{ab}\,t}.
\]

On the plane cut,

\[
|\zeta|^2=c^2.
\]

Hence the sign-safe statement is

\[
\boxed{|\zeta|=|c|.}
\]

The current manuscript writes \(|\zeta|=c\) without first imposing \(c>0\).  This is valid only after choosing the standard ellipse normalization \(a,b,c>0\).

For \(ab<0\),

\[
\eta_\pm=(a+b)X+(a-b)T\pm2\sqrt{-ab}\,Y
\]

satisfy

\[
\boxed{\eta_+\eta_-=c^2.}
\]

That identity is sign-safe as written.

## 7. [Audit] the proposed power map is normalization-dependent

This is the most important conceptual correction after the orbit-sheet issue.

The geometric line is unchanged if its equation is rescaled:

\[
(a,b,c)\mapsto(\kappa a,\kappa b,\kappa c),
\qquad \kappa\ne0.
\]

The generator rescales:

\[
G_{\kappa a,\kappa b}=\kappa G_{a,b},
\]

which changes only the flow parameter and therefore gives the same one-parameter subgroup **as an orbit set**.

But the eigen-coordinate also rescales (for positive \(\kappa\)):

\[
\zeta_{\kappa a,\kappa b}=\kappa\zeta_{a,b}.
\]

Raising to a power gives

\[
(\kappa\zeta)^n=\kappa^n\zeta^n.
\]

The manuscript then reconstructs a target line with right-hand side \((\kappa c)^n\).  Dividing the target equation by \(\kappa\) gives a geometric target level proportional to

\[
\kappa^{n-1}c^n.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the map }ax+by=c\mapsto ax+by=c^n
\text{ is not canonical for an unnormalized geometric line.}
}
\]

A normalization convention must be fixed first.

Possible publication-safe choices include:

1. normalize \(c=1\) when \(c\ne0\), in which case the power map acts on a normalized member rather than producing the claimed size ladder;
2. normalize \(a^2+b^2=1\) (or another coefficient norm), then the target size is well-defined relative to that convention;
3. explicitly state that the power map is attached to a **chosen equation** \((a,b,c)\), not canonically to the underlying geometric line.

The third option requires the least structural rewriting.

## 8. [Audit] the intertwining statement is salvageable, but the proof contains a false equality

For integer \(n\ge1\), if \(\Psi_n\) is defined by

\[
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^n,
\]

then the intended semiconjugacy is

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_n\circ\operatorname{flow}_t
=\operatorname{flow}_{nt}\circ\Psi_n.
}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\Psi_n(\zeta_0e^{\omega t})
=(\zeta_0e^{\omega t})^n
=\zeta_0^n e^{\omega nt},
\]

which equals the time-\(nt\) flow starting from \(\Psi_n(\zeta_0)=\zeta_0^n\).

The current proof instead writes an equality equivalent to

\[
\zeta_0e^{\omega nt}
=(\zeta_0e^{\omega t})^n,
\]

which is false unless \(\zeta_0^{n-1}=1\).

Thus the theorem statement can survive after normalization/domain cleanup, but the proof must be rewritten around the **output initial condition** \(\zeta_0^n\).

Also restrict the power exponent to a clear domain such as

\[
\boxed{n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}}
\]

unless branch choices for noninteger powers are explicitly introduced.

## 9. [Audit] hyperbolic power map needs an explicit reconstruction rule

The ellipse case explicitly reconstructs \((X_n,T_n)\) from two linear equations and obtains \(Y_n\) from the imaginary part.

The hyperbola case currently says it is “identical” after

\[
\eta_\pm\mapsto\eta_\pm^n,
\]

but does not state the corresponding reconstruction equations.

A publication-ready theorem should define, for example,

\[
Q_n=\frac{\eta_+^n+\eta_-^n}{2},
\qquad
Y_n=\frac{\eta_+^n-\eta_-^n}{4\sqrt{-ab}},
\]

and then solve

\[
(a+b)X_n+(a-b)T_n=Q_n,
\]

\[
(a-b)X_n+(a+b)T_n=c^n
\]

subject to the chosen normalization/sign convention.

Only after this definition should the reverse completed-square identity be invoked.

## 10. [Audit] “no power map for rows and columns” is false as stated

Paper B concludes that in the parabolic case \(ab=0\),

> there is no power map at all.

But the elementary coordinatewise map already used later/earlier in the manuscript,

\[
\boxed{
P_n(x,y)=(x^n,y^n),
}
\]

sends a row

\[
x=c
\]

to

\[
x=c^n,
\]

and a column \(y=c\) to \(y=c^n\).

Moreover, for every positive pair,

\[
P_n(x,y)
\mapsto
\left(
\frac{x^n-y^n}{2},
(xy)^{n/2},
\frac{x^n+y^n}{2}
\right)
\]

still lies on the AM--GM cone.

Therefore the strongest justified statement is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The parabolic generator has no nonzero eigen-coordinate of the elliptic/hyperbolic type.}
}
\]

Equivalently, the **eigen-coordinate power construction** degenerates at \(ab=0\).

It is not valid to infer that every power-map construction is impossible.

This correction also removes the manuscript's claim that the row/column case “resists this treatment entirely.”  It resists diagonalization, not all algebraic powering.

## 11. [Audit] literal translation is not the parabolic Lorentz flow

The current row/column discussion says the natural one-parameter family preserving a row is literal translation

\[
y\mapsto y+d.
\]

That operation preserves the row in factor coordinates, but it should not be conflated with

\[
\exp(tG_{a,0}),
\]

the nilpotent/parabolic Lorentz flow introduced by the manuscript itself.

The latter is a null rotation on the signed cone and is polynomial in \(t\) because \(G\) is nilpotent.  Its action on \((x,y)\) need not be literal affine translation in \(y\).

Publication-safe wording should distinguish these two parameterizations.

## 12. [Audit] “canonical” needs projective qualification

Scaling \((a,b)\) by a common nonzero factor scales \(G_{a,b}\) and reparametrizes time but does not change the subgroup orbit set.

Thus the subgroup is naturally attached to the projective coefficient ratio

\[
[a:b],
\]

or equivalently to the plane normal direction, not to a uniquely normalized matrix generator.

Recommended wording:

> each non-null cut direction determines a canonical one-parameter subgroup up to time rescaling.

This is stronger and more precise than calling the raw matrix \(G_{a,b}\) canonical without qualification.

## 13. Recommended Paper B architecture

The paper is salvageable and may become substantially stronger after narrowing the claims:

### Part I — Signed orbit geometry

1. Extend Paper A's cone temporarily to the signed future cone \(Y\in\mathbb R\).
2. Prove that each nondegenerate signed plane section is one \(G_{a,b}\)-orbit.
3. State explicitly that the multiplication-table image is the \(Y\ge0\) half.
4. Derive the Lorentz norm/eigenvalue classification.

### Part II — Eigen-coordinates

1. Normalize ellipse signs or write \(|\zeta|=|c|\).
2. Retain \(\eta_+\eta_-=c^2\).
3. Present this as the exact diagonalization of elliptic/hyperbolic flows.

### Part III — Chosen-equation power maps

1. Fix a normalization convention or explicitly attach the map to a chosen equation \((a,b,c)\).
2. Restrict \(n\) to positive integers.
3. Correct the semiconjugacy proof.
4. Give a complete hyperbolic reconstruction formula.

### Part IV — Parabolic boundary

1. State that diagonal eigen-coordinates collapse because the generator is nilpotent.
2. Do **not** claim no power map exists.
3. Compare the parabolic null-rotation flow with coordinatewise powering as two distinct constructions.

### Part V — Constant product and rapidity

Retain

\[
(x,y)\mapsto(x^n,y^n),
\qquad
s\mapsto ns,
\]

but note that this same coordinatewise map also acts on rows/columns and general factor sets; its special significance for constant product is that it sends \(xy=N\) to \(xy=N^n\).
