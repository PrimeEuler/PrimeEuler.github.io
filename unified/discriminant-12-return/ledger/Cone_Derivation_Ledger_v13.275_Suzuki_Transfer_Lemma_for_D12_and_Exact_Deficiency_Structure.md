## 3. The exact abstract transfer lemma

The crucial observation is that Suzuki's deficiency-index construction does not intrinsically require the Riemann zeta function once a positive finite-interval form operator has been obtained.

### Transfer Lemma

Let `A` be a self-adjoint lower-bounded operator associated with a closed finite-interval quadratic form on `L^2(-a,a)`. Choose

\[
\lambda<\inf\sigma(A)
\]

and define

\[
T=A-\lambda I>0.
\]

Assume:

1. `H_0^1(-a,a)` is a form core compatible with the derivative operator;
2. the energy completion with norm
   \[
   \|f\|_T^2=\langle Tf,f\rangle
   \]
   admits the continuous endpoint/boundary functionals used in Suzuki's integration-by-parts argument;
3. for every `z` off the real axis, the equation
   \[
   Tv_z=e^{-izx}
   \]
   has a unique energy-space solution.

Then the symmetric first-order operator

\[
\mathscr D=i\frac d{dx}
\]

with compactly supported/zero-boundary core has deficiency spaces generated by

\[
\boxed{v_+=T^{-1}e^x,\qquad v_-=T^{-1}e^{-x},}
\]

and, provided neither deficiency vector vanishes,

\[
\boxed{n_+(\mathscr D)=n_-(\mathscr D)=1.}
\]

More generally,

\[
\boxed{v_z=T^{-1}e^{-izx}}
\]

is the adjoint eigenvector associated with spectral parameter `z`.

### Proof mechanism

For a core vector `f`, the adjoint equation

\[
\langle \mathscr D f,v_z\rangle_T
=
\langle f,zv_z\rangle_T
\]

becomes, using `Tv_z=e^{-izx}`,

\[
\langle i f',e^{-izx}\rangle_{L^2}
=
\langle f,z e^{-izx}\rangle_{L^2},
\]

which is exactly integration by parts on the finite interval; the boundary term vanishes on the core. The scalar first-order ODE has a one-dimensional solution space, hence each deficiency space is at most one-dimensional. Existence of `T^{-1}e^{\pm x}` makes it exactly one-dimensional.

This isolates the genuinely transferable core of Suzuki's argument.

## 6. Lower boundedness is the right finite-interval target, not positivity

Suzuki's 2026 construction is deliberately unconditional: the finite localized operator is lower bounded, then shifted by

\[
\lambda<\inf\sigma(A_a)
\]

to obtain the positive energy operator `T_a`.

Therefore the D12 transfer should target:

\[
\boxed{
Q_{W,K}^a[f]
\ge
-C_{K,a}\|f\|_2^2
}
\]

for every fixed finite `a`, with finite `C_{K,a}`.

This is substantially weaker than demanding

\[
Q_{W,K}^a[f]\ge0.
\]

The prime part is especially manageable because for fixed `a` it is a finite sum of bounded shifts:

\[
V_{K,a}
=
-\sum_{\log n\le2a}
\frac{b_K(n)}{\sqrt n}
(S_{a,\log n}+S_{a,\log n}^*).

Since

\[
\|S_{a,v}\|\le1,

\[
\boxed{
\|V_{K,a}\|
\le
2\sum_{\log n\le2a}
\frac{b_K(n)}{\sqrt n}.
}
\]

Hence the non-archimedean D12 perturbation cannot destroy lower boundedness of any lower-bounded archimedean reference operator on a fixed interval.

This is a genuine reduction: **the finite-prime portion of the D12 Friedrichs theorem is already harmless from the lower-bound point of view.**

The remaining analytic issue is the archimedean/reference part and the precise identification of its closed form domain.
