# Cone Derivation Ledger v13.499 — Totatives and QR(mod 12) on the AM-GM Cone

## Scope

Test, in the elementary AM-GM cone of `PaperA_ConicTheorem_v2.4.tex`,
\[
\Pi_\pm(x,y)=\left(\frac{x-y}2,\pm\sqrt{xy},\frac{x+y}2\right),\qquad X^2+Y^2=T^2,
\]
two proposed connections to the discriminant-12 arithmetic already developed elsewhere in this ledger (v13.478 character table, v13.487/v13.491 A3 lattice work): (1) whether the cone's fixed-sum circles and tangent circles "directly show" the totatives \(\{1,5,7,11\}\bmod12\); (2) a proposed bijection between the quadratic residues mod 12 and the radius set \(\{1,3,4,6\}\).

Both are checked by direct computation, not assumed. One genuinely new derived fact is found (§3); one elementary but real fact is confirmed and separated from an arithmetic overlay (§1–2); one numeric bijection is confirmed exactly, with an explicit guardrail on how much motivation it currently has (§4).

No new Pell, Suzuki, or A3-representation theorem is asserted. This entry documents elementary number theory realized on the cone of the companion geometry paper.

## 1. The totatives on the \(K=12\) fixed-sum circle

By the paper's own anti-diagonal proposition, \(x+y=K\) projects to the circle
\[
X^2+Y^2=(K/2)^2
\]
at height \(T=K/2\). For \(K=12\) this is \(X^2+Y^2=36\) at \(T=6\), and it contains all 11 integer pairs \((x,12-x)\), \(x=1,\dots,11\) — not only the four coprime-to-12 ones. Direct check for every \(x\):
\[
X=\Bigl(\tfrac{x-(12-x)}2\Bigr)=x-6,\qquad Y^2=x(12-x),\qquad X^2+Y^2=(x-6)^2+x(12-x)=36
\]
identically in \(x\), so the circle contains the full antidiagonal, coprime or not. This is the generic anti-diagonal-circle fact of the paper; it carries no arithmetic content by itself.

Restricting to \(x\) coprime to 12 selects exactly \(\varphi(12)=4\) of the 11 points:
\[
(1,11),(5,7),(7,5),(11,1)\ \longmapsto\
(X,Y,T)\in\{(-5,\sqrt{11},6),(-1,\sqrt{35},6),(1,\sqrt{35},6),(5,\sqrt{11},6)\},
\]
each verified against \(X^2+Y^2=36\) directly (\(25+11=36\), \(1+35=36\)).

**Guardrail.** The cone's conic geometry does not detect coprimality; it is agnostic to which lattice points on a given line it contains. The totatives are singled out by an arithmetic filter (\(\gcd(x,12)=1\)) applied to the antidiagonal's integer points, not generated by the cone construction itself. "The cone directly shows the totatives" should be read as: *the multiplication table's own integer lattice, restricted by an outside coprimality condition, and then carried through the paper's own anti-diagonal-circle theorem, places the four totatives on one explicit circle* — true, but the arithmetic selection is external to the conic geometry.

## 2. Row/column tangent circles at the totatives

By the paper's tangent proposition, row \(x=u\) is tangent to \(C_{u/2}:X^2+Y^2=(u/2)^2\) at \((u/2,0)\), for every \(u=1,\dots,11\), again generically. At \(u\in\{1,5,7,11\}\) this gives four concentric circles of radii \(1/2,5/2,7/2,11/2\).

Sorting these four radii by the already-established \(\chi_{-3}\) character (v13.478 §1, QR mod 3: \(\chi_{-3}(1)=\chi_{-3}(7)=+1\), \(\chi_{-3}(5)=\chi_{-3}(11)=-1\)) splits them as
\[
\{1/2,\,7/2\}\ (\chi_{-3}=+1)\qquad\text{versus}\qquad\{5/2,\,11/2\}\ (\chi_{-3}=-1).
\]
This is a correct restatement of the existing character table in terms of the cone's tangent-circle radii; it supplies no new arithmetic beyond v13.478.

## 3. New fact: the antidiagonal pairing is forced by \(\chi_{12}\) evenness

The antidiagonal \(x+y=12\) pairs \(x\) with \(12-x\equiv -x\pmod{12}\). Among the totatives this gives exactly the two pairs found in §1: \(\{1,11\}\) and \(\{5,7\}\).

Compute:
\[
\chi_{-4}(-1)=\chi_{-4}(3)=-1,\qquad \chi_{-3}(-1)=\chi_{-3}(2)=-1,
\]
(both odd, as expected for characters of negative discriminant), so
\[
\chi_{12}(-1)=\chi_{-4}(-1)\chi_{-3}(-1)=(-1)(-1)=+1.
\]
Hence \(\chi_{12}\) is an **even** character, so \(\chi_{12}(12-x)=\chi_{12}(-x)=\chi_{12}(-1)\chi_{12}(x)=\chi_{12}(x)\) for every \(x\). Checked directly on the table (v13.478 §1): \(\chi_{12}(1)=\chi_{12}(11)=+1\) and \(\chi_{12}(5)=\chi_{12}(7)=-1\), matching the two antidiagonal pairs exactly.

\[
\boxed{\text{The }K=12\text{ antidiagonal's pairing }x\leftrightarrow12-x\text{ respects }\chi_{12}\text{-parity, because }\chi_{12}\text{ is even.}}
\]

This is the one place in this entry where the cone's own construction (the antidiagonal, which pairs \(x\) with \(K-x\) by definition) is forced to align with the discriminant-12 character data, rather than merely being restated alongside it: evenness of \(\chi_{12}\) is a structural fact about the real quadratic field \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt3)\) (positive discriminant \(\Rightarrow\) even real character), and it is exactly what makes the \(K=12\) antidiagonal's built-in involution \(x\mapsto12-x\) a \(\chi_{12}\)-symmetry rather than a \(\chi_{12}\)-mixing map.

## 4. QR(mod 12) and the radius set \(\{1,3,4,6\}\)

Direct computation of \(r^2\bmod12\) for \(r=0,\dots,11\):
\[
0,1,4,9,4,1,0,1,4,9,4,1
\]
so the quadratic-residue set is
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{QR}(12)=\{0,1,4,9\}.}
\]
This matches the claimed set \(\{1,9,4,0\}\) exactly. (Note \((\mathbf Z/12)^*\cong V_4\) is 2-torsion, so among *units* every square is 1; the richer set \(\{0,1,4,9\}\) only appears when all of \(\mathbf Z/12\), not just the unit group, is squared — e.g. the diagonal cells \((u,u)\) of the multiplication table, whose products \(u^2\bmod12\) realize exactly this set.)

Testing the proposed radius set \(\{1,3,4,6\}\) against the tangent-circle proposition (row \(u\), radius \(u/2\), with row labels taken canonically in \(\{1,\dots,12\}\) since the residue-class picture is mod 12): define
\[
u(q)=\bigl(2q\bmod12\bigr),\qquad\text{using representative }12\text{ in place of }0,
\]
and radius \(=u(q)/2\). Direct computation:

| \(q\in\mathrm{QR}(12)\) | \(2q\bmod12\) | row label \(u\) | radius \(u/2\) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 2 | **1** |
| 9 | 18\(\to\)6 | 6 | **3** |
| 4 | 8 | 8 | **4** |
| 0 | 0 | 12 | **6** |

\[
\boxed{q\mapsto u(q)/2\ \text{sends}\ \{0,1,4,9\}\ \text{bijectively to}\ \{6,1,4,3\}=\{1,3,4,6\},}
\]
confirmed injective (images \(2,6,8,0\) are the four distinct residues), matching the claimed radius set exactly.

**Guardrail — this map is confirmed, not yet derived.** The verification above is an exact arithmetic check that the stated bijection reproduces the stated numbers; it is not yet a derivation of the doubling map \(q\mapsto2q\) from any previously established structure in this project. Two honest observations:

1. The reduction to *row labels mod 12* (rather than raw antidiagonal sums, which can run 1–24 in the unreduced 12×12 table) is natural given that this project works throughout in residues mod 12 — the tangent radius is then only meaningful mod 6, since halving an even class mod 12 is well-defined mod 6. That much is a reasonable framing choice, not an ad hoc one.
2. The specific choice of *doubling* \(q\) to get the row label (rather than, say, taking \(u=q\) directly, which would give the non-matching radii \(\{0,1/2,2,9/2\}\)) has no independent motivation yet established elsewhere in the ledger. It reproduces the claimed numbers exactly, but "reproduces the claimed numbers" is necessary, not sufficient, for a genuine mechanism — consistent with the standard already applied in this ledger to other proposed bridges (e.g. the \(\chi_{12}\)-phase-bridge thread's unfalsifiability guardrail, and the \(H^1=0\)/RH type-mismatch finding). Until an independent reason for the factor of 2 is found (e.g. from the cone's own \(\Pi_\pm\) map, or from the discriminant-12 Pell/tangent structure), this should be read as a confirmed numerical coincidence with a plausible but unproven arithmetic seed, not a theorem.
