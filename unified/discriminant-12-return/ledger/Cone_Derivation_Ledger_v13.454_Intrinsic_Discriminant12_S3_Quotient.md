## 1. Discriminant 12 forces the ramified primes 2 and 3 [D]

For the real quadratic field

\[
F=\mathbf Q(\sqrt3),
\qquad
\operatorname{disc}(F)=12=2^2\cdot3,
\]

the only ramified rational primes are

\[
\boxed{2\text{ and }3.}
\]

The discriminant-12 Pell unit is

\[
\lambda=2+\sqrt3,
\]

and the cyclotomic completion is

\[
K=\mathbf Q(\zeta_{12})=\mathbf Q(\sqrt3,i).
\]

Thus the two local reductions used in the preceding ledger are not arbitrary auxiliary primes: they are exactly the primes selected by the discriminant.

## 2. The prime-3 ramified tangent quotient [D]

In

\[
\mathcal O_F=\mathbf Z[\sqrt3],
\]

the prime 3 ramifies as

\[
(3)=(\sqrt3)^2.
\]

Set

\[
\epsilon:=\sqrt3\pmod3.
\]

Then

\[
\epsilon^2=0,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
R_3:=\mathcal O_F/3\mathcal O_F
\cong
\mathbf F_3[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2).
}
\]

Its unit group is

\[
R_3^\times
=
\mathbf F_3^\times\cdot(1+\epsilon\mathbf F_3).
\]

Quotienting by scalar units gives the canonical tangent phase group

\[
\boxed{
P_3:=R_3^\times/\mathbf F_3^\times
\cong1+\epsilon\mathbf F_3
\cong C_3.
}
\]

The Pell unit reduces as

\[
\lambda=2+\epsilon=-(1-\epsilon).
\]

Therefore its projective class is

\[
\boxed{[\lambda]=1-\epsilon\in P_3,}
\]

which has exact order 3 and generates `P_3`.

## 3. Prime-3 conjugation acts by inversion [D]

Real quadratic conjugation

\[
c:\sqrt3\mapsto-\sqrt3
\]

induces

\[
\epsilon\mapsto-\epsilon.
\]

Hence

\[
c(1-\epsilon)=1+\epsilon=(1-\epsilon)^{-1}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{cxc^{-1}=x^{-1}\qquad(x\in P_3).}
\]

The discriminant-12 prime-3 local phase group is therefore

\[
\boxed{
G_3:=P_3\rtimes\langle c\rangle
\cong C_3\rtimes_{-1}C_2
\cong S_3.
}
\]

This construction is intrinsic to the ramified local ring at 3 together with the nontrivial automorphism of `F/Q`.

## 4. Explicit Pell quotient map [D]

Let

\[
D_{\mathrm{Pell}}
:=
\langle \lambda,c\mid c^2=1,\ c\lambda c=\lambda^{-1}\rangle.
\]

This is the abstract infinite Pell inversion group before imposing any modulus.

Define

\[
\boxed{
\pi_3:D_{\mathrm{Pell}}\twoheadrightarrow G_3
}
\]

by

\[
\boxed{
\pi_3(\lambda)=1-\epsilon,
\qquad
\pi_3(c)=c.
}
\]

The defining inversion relation is preserved, and `1-epsilon` generates the normal `C3`. Hence `pi_3` is surjective.

Equivalently, on the integral Pell matrix realization,

\[
\boxed{
[g_{12}]\mapsto1-\epsilon,
\qquad
[J_f]\mapsto c,

after reduction modulo 3 and removal of scalar `F3^x`.

This is the canonical nonzero residue phase group at the ramified prime 2.

## 6. Prime-2 conjugation/Frobenius acts by inversion [D]

Complex conjugation is

\[
\sigma_{11}:\zeta_{12}\mapsto\zeta_{12}^{-1}.
\]

On the residue field it becomes Frobenius:

\[
\mathrm{Fr}(z)=z^2.
\]

Since `F4^x` has order 3,

\[
\omega^2=\omega^{-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{Fr}\,M_\omega\,\mathrm{Fr}^{-1}=M_{\omega^{-1}}.
}
\]

Thus the discriminant-12 prime-2 local phase group is

\[
\boxed{
G_2:=P_2\rtimes\langle\mathrm{Fr}\rangle
\cong C_3\rtimes_{-1}C_2
\cong S_3.
}
\]

Again this is intrinsic to the ramified local residue structure and its nontrivial residue-field automorphism.

## 9. Intrinsic discriminant-12 characterization [D/I]

The preceding construction uses only:

1. the discriminant-12 real quadratic field `Q(sqrt3)`;
2. its ramified prime 3 and the projectivized first-order local unit group;
3. the discriminant-12 cyclotomic completion `Q(zeta_12)`;
4. its ramified prime 2 and nonzero residue-field unit group;
5. the natural involutions induced by conjugation.

Each ramified prime produces canonically an order-three phase carrier:

\[
\boxed{
P_3=(\mathcal O_F/3)^\times/\mathbf F_3^\times\cong C_3,
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
P_2=(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak P_2)^\times\cong\mathbf F_4^\times\cong C_3.
}
\]

In each case the distinguished involution acts nontrivially, hence by the only nontrivial automorphism of `C3`, namely inversion.

Therefore the local extension is forced:

\[
\boxed{
C_3\rtimes_{-1}C_2\cong S_3.
}
\]

So the common `S3` does not need to be discovered by post hoc comparison of two large groups. It is already forced independently at each ramified prime by discriminant 12.

There are only two homomorphisms

\[

\[
\boxed{

This gives an intrinsic uniqueness characterization of the shared finite phase symmetry.

## 13. Guardrails [Audit]

- The prime-2 and prime-3 local carriers remain different rings in different characteristics.
- `Theta_23` is a group/phase intertwiner, not a ring isomorphism.
- The choice of rotation orientation can be reversed simultaneously (`r -> r^{-1}`); this changes the generator labeling but not the intrinsic `S3` extension.
- The result does not identify geometric Cone adjacency with group multiplication.
- The result does not identify Suzuki operator blocks with these local phase groups.
- The full `V4=U(12)` Galois structure contains more information than the terminal `S3` phase quotient; the two structures must not be conflated.

**Checkpoint conclusion.** The common finite `S3` phase symmetry is intrinsic to discriminant 12. The ramified prime 3 produces the projectivized tangent phase `P3=(O_F/3)^x/F3^x ~= C3`; the ramified prime 2 produces the residue phase `P2=F4^x ~= C3`. In both cases the natural discriminant involution acts by inversion, forcing the unique nonabelian semidirect product `C3 rt C2 ~= S3`. The explicit quotient maps send `lambda -> 1-epsilon -> omega` and quadratic/cyclotomic conjugation to inversion/Frobenius. Combined with v13.450, this shows that `S3` is both locally forced and the maximal common persistent phase quotient of the two ramified towers.
