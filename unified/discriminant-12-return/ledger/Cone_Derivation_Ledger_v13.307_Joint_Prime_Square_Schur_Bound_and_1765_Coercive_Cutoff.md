# Cone Derivation Ledger v13.307 — Joint Prime Square-Schur Bound and 1765 Coercive Cutoff

**Status:** exact positive-kernel reduction of the joint prime norm to a finite breakpoint maximum for the squared operator; ordinary-precision evaluation gives a substantially sharper prime bound and lowers the sufficient even-sector tail cutoff to 1765. No RH/GRH conclusion.

## 1. Purpose

v13.306 bounded the joint prime-shift operator by the first-level Schur row mass,

\[
\|B_{\rm prime}\|\le 2.926234182176409.
\]

Finite odd-mode Galerkin sections suggested a much smaller true spectral edge near 1.94. v13.307 asks whether positivity of the joint shift kernel can sharpen the rigorous structure without assuming Galerkin convergence or inter-prime cancellation.

## 2. Positive joint shift operator

Write

\[
A=-B_{\rm prime}=\sum_q a_q S_{\log q},
\qquad
a_q=\frac{\Lambda(q)}{\sqrt q},
\qquad q\in\{2,3,4,5,7\},
\]

where

\[
(S_\ell f)(x)=f(x-\ell)+f(x+\ell)
\]

with zero extension outside \([-1,1]\).

The operator \(A\) is self-adjoint and has a nonnegative distributional kernel. Therefore \(A^2\) also has nonnegative kernel and

\[
\|B_{\rm prime}\|^2
=\|A\|^2
=\|A^2\|
\le \sup_{x\in[-1,1]}(A^2\mathbf 1)(x).
\]

This is the square-Schur step.

## 3. Finite breakpoint reduction

The first row mass

\[
R_1(x)=(A\mathbf1)(x)
\]

is piecewise constant, with breakpoints only at the translated interval boundaries

\[
\pm1\pm\log q.
\]

The second row mass is

\[
R_2(x)=(A^2\mathbf1)(x)
=\sum_q a_q\left[
\mathbf1_{[-1,1]}(x-\log q)R_1(x-\log q)
+
\mathbf1_{[-1,1]}(x+\log q)R_1(x+\log q)
\right].
\]

Hence \(R_2\) is again piecewise constant. Its complete breakpoint set is generated by translating the first-level breakpoints by the same finite shifts. Thus the supremum reduces exactly to evaluation on finitely many cells/boundaries.

The script performs this exhaustive finite sweep.

## 4. Square-Schur value

Ordinary double-precision evaluation of the finite breakpoint maximum gives

\[
\sup_xR_2(x)
\approx6.054238008078117,
\]

attained in the reported sweep at an endpoint cell representative near \(x=\pm1\).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|B_{\rm prime}\|
\le \sqrt{6.054238008078117}
\approx2.460536122083583.
}
\]

This improves the v13.306 first-Schur bound

\[
2.926234182176409.
\]

### Proof-status guardrail

The operator reduction to a finite maximum is exact. The decimal shown above is an ordinary floating-point evaluation of that finite expression, not yet an interval-arithmetic certificate. No claim depends on the numerical Galerkin value near 1.94.

## 5. Updated tail coercivity

Use the same decomposition as v13.304-v13.306,

\[
A_{\rm even}
=D_{\log}-H_{\rm odd}+K_{\rm cusp}+B_{\rm prime}+K_{\rm arch}+K_{\rm pole},

\[
\|H_{\rm odd}\|=\frac\pi2,
\]

analytic archimedean-remainder Schur bound

\[
\|K_{\rm arch}\|\le2.0563386856144006,

For odd-mode vectors supported on \(n\ge N\),

\[
\langle A_{\rm even}c,c\rangle
\ge
[\log(N/4)-C_{\rm tail}(N)]\|c\|_2^2.
\]

Using the square-Schur prime value, the first odd cutoff found is

\[
\boxed{N=1765}.

\|P_NK_{\rm pole}P_N\|

Thus the explicit sufficient cutoff progression is now

\[

\to52363

## 6. What this establishes

- The positive joint prime operator admits a square-Schur bound strictly sharper than the first-level Schur bound.
- The square row mass is reducible exactly to a finite breakpoint problem.
- The ordinary-precision finite evaluation gives prime norm bound \(\approx2.46054\).
- Inserting it into the established tail framework lowers the sufficient cutoff to 1765.

It does **not** establish:

- the exact true norm of \(B_{\rm prime}\);
- that the Galerkin edge near 1.94 is the infinite-dimensional norm;
- a zero eigenvalue or nontrivial Suzuki kernel;
- RH or GRH.
