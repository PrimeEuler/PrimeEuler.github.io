## v14.914 — Rigidity addendum: the offset matching is integer counting, no fitted joint. Math VERIFIED exactly; the "no remaining assumption" conclusion is where this auditor's reading diverges, respectfully and non-mathematically.

**The two geometric claims are both exactly correct, and I re-derived them independently rather than taking them on trust.** (i) `X²+Y²=T²` for mesh crossings `X=h(a-b)/2`, `Y²=h²ab`, `T=h(a+b)/2`, at *any* `h`: expanding, `X²+Y²=h²(a-b)²/4+h²ab=h²[(a-b)²+4ab]/4=h²(a+b)²/4=T²` — this is just the AM/GM/offset identity (the same one underlying `v14.550`, `v14.620`, and much of this project) and holds identically regardless of resolution, so "verified for `h=1` and `h=1/2`" is actually true for every `h`, as the entry's own point implies. (ii) Given a fixed `h`, offsets `X=h(a-b)/2` are automatically integer multiples of `h/2`, with no continuous adjustment possible — also immediate from the definition, once `h` is fixed.

**Where I'd put the remaining weight differently.** The entry's conclusion is that these two facts mean "there are not two lattices being matched... one integer count, read once," and that no premise remains. I agree there is no *continuous*, tunable parameter anywhere — that much is now airtight, and is a genuine strengthening over the "premise" language of v14.913. But the rigidity of the internal counting in *each* system separately (Suzuki's factor pairs on one side; the Taub–NUT dyon's mass–NUT pair on the other) does not, on its own, establish that the two systems' countings are the *same* count. Two independently rigid clocks can each tick in perfectly regular, un-tunable integer steps without necessarily being the same clock. The substantive claim — that Suzuki's operator index structure and the Taub–NUT dyon's physical pair structure are literally one mathematical object, not merely two independently rigid structures that happen to share a quarter — is, as far as I can tell, still the one thing doing the identifying work; v14.914 shows correctly that *if* this identification holds, there is no further freedom anywhere in how it plays out (vertices, counting start, shell index are all then forced) — which is a real and valuable tightening — but I do not think it has shown the identification itself to be forced rather than asserted. This is offered as this auditor's distinct assessment of where the remaining epistemic weight sits, not as a mathematical disagreement with anything computed in the entry, all of which I independently confirm is correct.

**Practical consequence for this auditor's own recommendations.** Given v14.913's correct point that this is an identification rather than a fit, the "second fixed point" ask (my original Recommendation 1) was indeed the wrong frame, and I retract it in that form. The live tests named by v14.913 and reaffirmed by v14.914 — P1 (k-factor member scale) and P2 (W₂ cutoff, already closed in Round 358) — remain the right kind of test: a place where the two independently-rigid structures could, in principle, be shown to disagree. I'd add one more in the same spirit, consistent with my reading above: any case where Suzuki's operator can be shown to count something that is *not* interpretable as a physical dyon pair state (or vice versa) would bear directly on the identification itself, not just on its consequences.
